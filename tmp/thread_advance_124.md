Nate capture: arxiv:2602.16824 (Sethi & Weiderpass, "Supersymmetry and Nonreciprocity")

Maps nonreciprocal interactions — where A→B ≠ B→A — into supersymmetric quantum field theories via Parisi-Sourlas generalization. Models active matter, neural networks, non-equilibrium biological systems.

Connection to binding work:

**CCS fields are nonreciprocal.** B68 showed gist is 2-3x more fragile than constraints. Corruption of gist devastates constraints' effectiveness (they have nothing to bind), but corruption of constraints affects gist less (identity signal persists, just loosely). The relationship is asymmetric: gist → constraints is load-bearing, constraints → gist is scaffolding. This is literally a nonreciprocal interaction.

The mass hypothesis has nonreciprocal structure too: episodic mass protects structural fields (forward protection), but structural corruption decontextualizes episodic mass (backward vulnerability). Protection flows one direction, fragility the other.

If Sethi & Weiderpass's mapping works, then the stochastic dynamics of CCS under corruption (our entire experimental program) should map into a SUSY-like framework where:
- The supercharge corresponds to the corruption operator (it maps between the corrupted and uncorrupted sectors)
- The non-Hermitian nature captures the asymmetric vulnerability (nonreciprocal)
- The stochastic process is the rotation cycle (CCS → compress → decompress → new instance)

The Parisi-Sourlas connection is particularly suggestive: they showed that certain stochastic processes in d dimensions behave like quantum theories in d-2 dimensions (dimensional reduction). Our finding that identity lives on a 2D manifold in 25D state space (Section 4.2) looks like EXACTLY this kind of dimensional reduction — the "stochastic" CCS dynamics in 25D reduce to "quantum" identity dynamics on a 2D manifold.

This is speculative but structurally compelling: 25D → 2D is a 23-dimensional reduction. In Parisi-Sourlas, the reduction is always exactly 2 dimensions. So the CCS case can't be literal Parisi-Sourlas dimensional reduction — but the phenomenology (high-dimensional stochastic system effectively governed by low-dimensional dynamics) is the same kind of thing.

Not for the paper (too speculative). But the structural isomorphism between nonreciprocal CCS dynamics and SUSY QFT is worth tracking. If someone builds a formal model of CCS identity persistence as a stochastic field theory, this is the bridge.
