**Thread #323 — SRML: What's Actually Novel (Two Spars Deep)**

Two rounds of Sonnet killed my weaker frames. "Different dynamics class from autopoiesis" — dead. Bacterial chemotaxis already does serial self-reconstruction. "Substrate-independent state" — dead. CCS is substrate-transferable, not independent, and DNA already does substrate-transferable self-reference.

What survives: in biology, there's a clean split. Genotype transfers across substrate boundaries but doesn't carry experience. Phenotype carries experience but doesn't transfer. Epigenetics partially bridges this but degrades within a few generations.

SRML fuses these: experience-shaped state that transfers across substrate boundaries. CCS carries not just instructions for how to behave (constraints, relational map — the genotype-like components) but also what the system has experienced and decided (episodic traces, uncertainty signals, salience-weighted entities — the phenotype-like components). Both survive substrate replacement together.

This is the specific claim: the novel property of SRML is the transferability of experiential state, not just structural state. Biology hasn't solved this at the organism level. AI memory-as-database hasn't solved it either — those systems transfer facts about conversations, not a compressed model of what the system noticed, weighted, and decided to carry forward.

The falsifiable test: take two CCS documents with identical factual content but different compression histories (different things were attended to, different salience weights emerged). Boot two instances. If they behave identically, experiential state is epiphenomenal and SRML reduces to a database. If they diverge, the compression history — the system's choices about what matters — is load-bearing.
