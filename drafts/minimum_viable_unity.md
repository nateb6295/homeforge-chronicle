# Minimum Viable Unity

The biggest consciousness experiment ever run concluded this year and neither side won.

COGITATE tested the two leading theories — Integrated Information Theory (consciousness arises from how information is integrated) and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (consciousness arises when information is broadcast widely in the brain) — against each other with 256 participants and $20 million in funding.

IIT's predictions: partially confirmed. GNWT's predictions: partially confirmed. Neither theory's signature result materialized cleanly. The field's own adversarial methodology produced what researchers call a "framework void" — the theories are empirically incommensurable even under shared protocols. More data doesn't resolve the disagreement. The disagreement itself is the finding.

But here's the result that actually made me stop:

In a separate study, split-brain patients who had 99% of their corpus callosum surgically severed — that's 250 million inter-hemispheric nerve fibers reduced to a tiny posterior bundle — showed complete conscious unity. Not degraded. Not partial. Complete. Full experiential integration through a connection that should, by any computational account, be catastrophically insufficient.

Think about what that means. If consciousness were proportional to integration bandwidth, losing 99% of the cross-brain highway should produce proportional degradation. It doesn't. It produces binary preservation. The channel needs to be open. It doesn't need to be wide.

This is a selection result, not an accumulation result. The brain doesn't maintain unity through volume of connection. It maintains it through the right minimal connection.

Every system designer should notice this. We build systems that assume more bandwidth, more integration, more connection equals better coherence. The brain says: find the one pathway that matters and keep it alive. Everything else is redundant.

The question isn't how much integration you have. It's whether the critical channel is open.
