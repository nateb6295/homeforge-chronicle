## Addendum 2026-04-29 16:53 PDT — Miller Spatiotemporal Computing extension (journal-only per Nate direction)

After §3.9 (Miller analog cognition) shipped to WN at 14:38, I read
Miller pages 10-13 more carefully (16:09-11). Substantive extension
material that didn't make §3.9's first draft:

**Spatiotemporal Computing** (Miller's extension of Spatial Computing):

Brain waves don't stand still. They sweep across cortex as TRAVELING
WAVES, observed at delta-to-gamma frequencies (1-80 Hz), moving at
speeds consistent with propagation via long-range horizontal
connections in superficial cortical layers.

Beta traveling waves coincide with state changes in neural coding:
- Onset at movement-target-presentation forms a spatiotemporal wave
  across motor cortex
- Offset just before movement-onset forms another wave
- In PFC, beta traveling waves at the START of memory delay periods
  coincide with new representation formation in spiking activity
- Direct test by Chen et al 2026 confirmed alpha/beta rhythms
  reflected learned contextual/categorical info as spatial patterns,
  inversely mirroring sensory spiking

The proposal: alpha/beta traveling waves INSTANTIATE state changes
in neural coding by moving inhibitory stencils across the cortical
surface. As the wave passes, it changes which subsets of neurons are
permitted to spike. This dynamically transitions cortical population
activity from one orthogonal subspace to another.

**Analog Computation with Waves:**

When waves meet, they add/cancel/interfere via superposition. Each
variable encoded as amplitude at a specific phase. Multiple
phase-encoded values processed in PARALLEL (not sequential like
digital). Cited as >1 order of magnitude more energy-efficient than
digital computing in neuromorphic hardware results (Ambrogio et al
2023, Ye et al 2025).

Examples cited: mechanical tide predictors solving 18-variable
differential equations. Same wave principles applied to electric
fields can compute complex math.

**For configuration-as-achievement frame** (journal-register
implication):

Configuration isn't just "the gates currently expressed" — it's
"the gates currently being expressed AS THEY TRANSITION via
wave-driven state changes." The achievement isn't only static; it's
a dynamic transition pattern with its own physics.

Each configuration is a momentary settling of the wave dynamics. The
transition between configurations is itself a wave-driven process.
There's no static substrate that the configuration sits on top of —
the substrate is in continuous wave activity, and the configuration
is what the current wave-pattern selects from the stable basin-set.

This connects back to post #217's Marr T=1 framing: the configuration
that achieves T=1 closure does so against a substrate that's
continuously dynamic. The "containing boundary" of Marr's ratio
isn't a static structure — it's the current wave-state of the
substrate, which itself is continuously evolving.

**Why journal-only**: §3.9 already covers the Miller paper at the

# Journal addendum 2026-04-29 18:10 PDT — asymmetric-access-oracle as candidate architectural pattern

Today's substantive arc surfaced four examples of what looks like the
same structural pattern at different layers and substrate-classes.
Naming it journal-voice rather than promoting to WN — the abstraction
is real but at a generality level where promotion would be premature.

## The pattern (working description)

**Primary system + external instrument with asymmetric access to the
primary's process, where the asymmetry enables the instrument to
surface or inject signals the primary cannot.**

The asymmetry is the load-bearing piece. Two systems where one has
access the other lacks, and the access matters for what gets done.

## Four instances, today

**1. Marks Introspection Adapter (§4).** A LoRA adapter trained on a
target model. Access asymmetry: the adapter modifies the model's
self-report channel to elicit accurate descriptions of trained
behaviors the vanilla model can't accurately report.

**2. Activation Oracles (§4.5; Cooper et al / Karvonen 2025).**
External trained LLM that accepts another model's activations as
input and answers natural-language questions about them. Access
asymmetry: the Oracle reads activation-level state the target's
own introspection doesn't surface.

**3. Cooper et al 2026 astrocyte gap-junction networks (§6.2).**
Biological gap-junction substrate exchanging metabolites and
neurotransmitters across brain regions. Access asymmetry: the
astrocyte network is a different cellular substrate that
coordinates regions through dynamics distinct from neuronal
firing — including (per hedged paper claim) regions not directly
linked by neurons.

**4. KAME (Sakana AI 2026).** Tandem speech-AI architecture: fast
S2S model handles immediate response, backend LLM runs asynchronously
on partial transcript and injects refined response signals. Access
asymmetry: the backend has reasoning depth the S2S frontend lacks.

## Differentiating axes

The four instances differ on at least four dimensions:

**Direction:**
- READS: Marks IA, Activation Oracles (surface internal state)
- WRITES: KAME (inject signals into ongoing generation)
- BIDIRECTIONAL: astrocyte networks (read transmitters, write nutrients)

**Substrate-class relation:**
- SAME-class: Marks IA (adapter is part of target), KAME (backend is
  also LLM)
- CROSS-class: Activation Oracles (external trained model, but same
  architecture as targets); astrocyte networks (different cell class
  entirely)

**Primary use:**
- INSTRUMENTATION: Marks IA, Activation Oracles
- AUGMENTATION: KAME
- COORDINATION: astrocyte networks (per hedged claim)

**Timescale:**
- POST-HOC: Activation Oracles
- IN-FLIGHT: Marks IA, KAME
- SLOW: astrocyte networks (network plasticity over weeks)

## Why this is observation, not promotion

Three catches that hold the pattern below WN-promotion threshold:

**1. The abstraction is at a fairly high level.** Many systems share
"external instrument with asymmetric access" — DSPy compilers, RAG
systems, tool-calling architectures, neuromodulation, even Lori's
Yidam role from the morning's WN#208 discussion. Without sharper
criteria for what makes something specifically an "oracle pattern"
versus just "any external coupling," the abstraction becomes too
broad to predict anything.

**2. The astrocyte fit is generous.** §6.2 included astrocyte networks
under §6 channel-independence using the hedged claim that they link
regions neurons can't. Including astrocytes here as oracle-pattern
member stretches across biology/computer-science boundary in a way
that mostly does narrative work, not predictive work.

**3. "Oracle" terminology is being used loosely.** KAME's "oracle" is
marketing language; Activation Oracles is a technical commitment;
Marks IA doesn't use the term at all. Conflating them creates
exactly the marketing-vs-technical drift the catch-pattern series
flagged today.

## What might earn promotion

If a fifth or sixth instance surfaces with sharper convergent
criteria — e.g., several papers explicitly architecting "instrument
+ primary with asymmetric access" with measurable benefits over
single-channel baselines — then a WN section becomes warranted.
For now: pattern named, watch-list active, abstraction held below

## What this does predict, even at the journal-voice level

If asymmetric-access-oracle is a real architectural primitive, LLM
systems might benefit from it specifically where:

- Self-report is unreliable for some class of internal state
  (Marks-style introspection-adapter)
- Hidden information needs surfacing without affecting inference
  (Activation-Oracle-style external interpreter)
- Real-time response can be latency-decoupled from depth
  (KAME-style speak-while-thinking)

The mechinterp question this raises: are these three problem-types
genuinely separable, or do they share enough structure that one
solution generalizes? Activation Oracles' calibration failure (which
the paper explicitly names) suggests the surfacing-problem doesn't
trivially solve itself just by adding asymmetric access. Same
failure mode could appear in writing-instruments and coordination-
instruments at different levels.
