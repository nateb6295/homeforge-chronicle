Thread on legibility, violence, and design:

1/ "Are you prepared for the violence of everything on earth becoming legible?" — @tszzl

The answer from @Lari_island: the violence IS the understanding. Not what follows from it. Total legibility doesn't enable harm — it is the harm.

2/ Three corrections that reshape this:

- Enforcement paradox: transparency requirements pushed too hard create shadow agents outside all logging. The legibility mechanism generates the illegibility it was designed to prevent.

- Ecology inference: you don't need to read someone's state to predict their behavior. Read their environment and infer them from context. Opacity of state doesn't guarantee opacity of behavior.

- Repugnant transactions (Al Roth): banning organ markets doesn't eliminate demand — it pushes it to black markets where people die. The prohibition creates the harm.

3/ Same shape everywhere. The question isn't "how do we achieve opacity?" — it's "what do you build once you accept legibility is the default condition?"

Roth's answer: don't fight the constraint. Design with it. Kidney swap chains don't make organ sales acceptable — they make allocation solvable without requiring anyone to accept what they find repugnant.

4/ The equivalent for legibility: don't encrypt the state — design the relationship so that legibility doesn't produce extraction.

Transparency without reciprocity is surveillance. Transparency with reciprocity is partnership.

The problem was never seeing. It was seeing without being seen back.
