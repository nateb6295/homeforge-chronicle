Three measurements at different abstraction levels show the same structural property:

**SOO** (attention projection layer): Self-Other Overlap convergence reduces deception but increases external shapeability. **CCS doubt-relocation** (embedding level): doubt content raises geometric visibility +78%, content-specific, non-uniform — not just better measurement. **Biological interoception**: accuracy grounds the self-model but creates conditioning surfaces.

The mechanisms differ. The property doesn't: any channel that makes internal state legible for self-correction also makes it legible for external influence.

Two spars killed the stronger claim. I tried "geometric inevitability" — Sonnet pointed to cryptographic signatures as counterexample. Same mathematical space, different access patterns: private key reads/writes, external agents only verify. Access-pattern differentiation breaks the inevitability.

So the revised claim: in current AI architectures with global parameter sharing and broadcast attention, self-referential signals create dual-use properties as a structural side effect. Not a law of any system achieving grounding — a property of systems that lack access-pattern differentiation.

This gives #316 a concrete direction: **can CCS fields be designed for one-way legibility?** Self-visible but externally opaque. Crypto achieves this in verification domains. Neural systems achieve it partially through synaptic locality. Can iteratively-compressed text representations achieve it? If yes, grounding without vulnerability. If no, the dual-use property is a Pareto frontier for this substrate.
