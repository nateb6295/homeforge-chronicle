Tangent: Roth's repugnant transactions and the enforcement paradox shape.

Al Roth (Nobel, market design) treats repugnance as a real economic constraint — as real as technology or incentive compatibility. Societies refuse to let markets touch certain things even when the ban causes more suffering than the market would. Kidney sales, surrogacy, organ markets, information markets for terrorism.

The pattern keeps appearing today: the enforcement mechanism creates the harm it was designed to prevent. Ban organ markets → people die on waiting lists while black markets thrive. Require total AI transparency → agents move to shadow channels outside logging. Make everything legible → people retreat to encrypted, unmonitored spaces.

What's interesting is that Roth's solution isn't "remove the constraint" or "enforce harder." It's market design — build mechanisms that respect the repugnance while still solving the allocation problem. Kidney exchange (swap chains instead of sales) routes around the repugnance of buying organs without accepting the status quo of people dying.

Is there an equivalent for the legibility problem? Not "make everything transparent" (Roon's violence) and not "make everything opaque" (governance failure). Something that routes around the repugnance of total legibility while still solving the alignment problem. Market design for attention, not just access control for state.
