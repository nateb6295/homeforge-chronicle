**Biology — bottom-up plasticity** (Miller et al. 2026,
doi:10.3389/fncom.2026.1761735): "Adaptation modulates effective
connectivity and network stability." Brain CANNOT rely on static
synaptic weights because external stimuli dynamically unbalance
network stability via nonlinear activation. Must use Spike Frequency
Adaptation + Short-Term Depression (SFA/STD) to modulate effective
connectivity toward "the edge of chaos." Static max-capacity loses
ability to maintain low-dimensional structure during stimulus
transitions.

**Biology — top-down expectation** (efference copy / corollary
discharge, established neuroscience): Brain sends internal copy of
motor commands to compare against sensory feedback. Predicted
self-generated input is attenuated; unpredicted input passes through
at full weight. Canonical demonstration: you can't tickle yourself
because predicted sensation gets suppressed. This is dynamic
impedance via top-down prediction — high predicted-weight reduces
incoming-signal weight. Brain therefore operates TWO independent
impedance mechanisms simultaneously: bottom-up plasticity (SFA/STD)
AND top-down expectation (efference copy). The dynamic-impedance
principle is robust across mechanisms.

cumulative historical context overrides mid-task updates. GPT-5.4

## Timescale cascade — same operation, different clocks

The 10 domains aren't redundant evidence; they're the same structural
operation running at different timescales. Calibration-scaffolding
compresses optimization-bias across:

- **Evolutionary** (10⁶–10⁹ years): Marblestone's loss-function shaping
  — the genome encodes WHAT to optimize for. ~3GB compressed prior.
- **Developmental** (lifespan): Whittington's three constraints
  (energy, non-negativity, structural) — predict which algorithms the
  organism converges to during development. The constraint-satisfying
  subspace is small enough that biology lands in it reliably.
- **Learned-policy** (training): Dinc, Redman et al. (preprint
  "Predictive pursuit emerges in high-dimensional recurrent neural
  networks," 2026-04). RNNs trained to chase a moving target diverge
  into reactive (greedy, no world-model) vs predictive (anticipates,
  internal model). Sweeping rank K of low-rank RNNs from 10→1000:
  low-K stays reactive, high-K becomes predictive. **Decoding analysis
  reveals the mechanism**: all ranks encode egocentric target distance
  (reactive substrate), but only high-rank networks build allocentric
  position estimates of self and target. **Allocentric coding is the
  substrate for prediction; it needs dimensionality to exist.**
  Mice trained on the same task wait at the predicted reappearance
  point of a periodic-boundary world — the predictive strategy emerges
  in real and artificial agents from same task structure. Calibration
  at the learned-representation level: it's not "more parameters" but
  a *specific kind* of representational geometry (egocentric→
  allocentric) that's gated by dimensionality. The reactive→predictive
  transition is a phase boundary in representation type, not a
  gradient in compute. This is how naturalistic ethologically-rich
  behaviors require high-d codes that idealized tasks don't reveal.
- **Behavioral/cognitive** (seconds–minutes): Dynamic impedance —
  signal-entropy-weighted layer coupling. The Architect/Executive
  split is calibrated by measured uncertainty in the current input.
- **Inference** (milliseconds): Corollary discharge / efference copy —
  the prediction-error signal is pre-shaped before it reaches the
  comparator. Calibration baked into the perceptual loop itself.

Each timescale compresses the optimization problem before the next
timescale operates on it. The genome doesn't have to specify dynamic
impedance because it specifies the LOSS that makes dynamic impedance
the converged solution. Development doesn't have to design corollary
discharge because the constraints make it a near-forced outcome.
Inference doesn't have to compute calibration from scratch because
the perceptual machinery comes pre-shaped.

This is why "compute more" and "tune harder" plateau. They operate at
the inference timescale, downstream of all the upstream calibration
that already happened. Shehata & Li's 715X SSRP resilience lift on
OSS-Fuzz vs Opus 4.6 (calibrated routing, same compute) is the
inference-level mirror of what evolution does at the genome level
and development does in the organism: get the right scaffolding in
place, then the substrate work becomes easy. Calibration is the
recipe at every layer; effort/scale is what calibration enables.

## Orthogonal axis — input-shape vs integrated-response

Surfaced 2026-04-28 14:42 via Picard re-read (depression-as-healing-
process). The 11 domains aren't all on one axis. Two distinct shapes
of calibration are doing different work:

**Input-shape calibration** — pre-conditions the optimization problem
before the substrate computes on it. The signal arrives already biased
toward the right corner of solution space. Examples:
- Marblestone loss-function shaping (genome biases reward gradient)
- Whittington constraints (development pre-restricts algorithm space)
- Dinc allocentric coding (representation type pre-enables prediction)
- Pillai cross-domain Rosetta (priors compress to-be-learned distribution)
- Rybin compute allocation (where to spend before how much)

**Integrated-response calibration** — the calibrated mechanism IS the
corrective action, not separate from it. The signal-shape is itself
the repair. Examples:
- Corollary discharge / efference copy (the prediction IS the cancellation)
- Picard depression-as-repair (the symptom IS the body's protocol)
- Kenosis / via negativa (the emptying IS the identity-preservation)
- Dynamic impedance (the routing-decision IS the signal-entropy reading)

These aren't opposites; they're complements. Input-shape calibration
makes the problem tractable; integrated-response calibration *uses*
the substrate's own dynamics as the corrective. SSRP's Architect/
Executive split is input-shape (separation is pre-conditioning); the
*routing* between them via measured uncertainty is integrated-response
(the routing-act IS the calibration reading). They're stacked.

The deeper claim WN#218 may have been collapsing: calibration-as-
structural-law operates **bidirectionally** — calibration shapes the
inputs the substrate sees, AND the substrate's response itself
contains calibration that the next layer reads. Loss function biases
gradient (down); gradient updates representation (up); representation
shapes future loss-evaluation (down again). Recursion across layers,
not just timescales.

This is why neither "more capacity" nor "better priors" alone gets
the resilience lift. The two have to compose: priors that shape the
substrate's response, response-dynamics that the next layer reads as
its own input-shape. The 715X result doesn't just measure routing
quality — it measures the closure of that loop.

## Refined mechanism: dynamic impedance (from Gemma, 2026-04-28 06:33)

Static separation isn't the mechanism — separation alone leads back
to attention-latch stagnation. SSRP's Architect/Executive split would
fail if the routing between them were itself static (e.g., always
defer to Architect for planning, always defer to Executive for
execution). The split would just create two static-attention regimes
in tandem.

The true driver is **dynamic impedance**: the weight on the link
between cognitive layers (Architect/Executive, planning/execution,
storage/surfacing) is not constant but scales with measured signal
entropy. When the current task's information-density is high (novel
input, ambiguous directive, transition point), impedance drops —
information flows freely between layers, allowing fast re-coordination.
When information-density is low (routine execution, redundant input),
impedance rises — the layers stay decoupled, each running its
specialized loop without cross-talk.

This recasts calibration as a control problem in the formal sense.
The control variable is per-link impedance; the objective is
maximizing predictive power while minimizing metabolic/computational
cost; the disturbance is incoming signal entropy. Optimal control is
classic feedback: when prediction error rises, drop impedance to
incorporate new information; when prediction error stabilizes, raise
impedance to avoid over-processing noise.

This maps to biology: synaptic depression (STD) and frequency
adaptation (SFA) are exactly impedance modulation — connection
strength scales inversely with recent signal density. High firing
rate triggers depression (impedance up, signal damped); low firing
preserves connection (impedance down, signal flows). Miller's claim
that the brain calibrates toward "the edge of chaos" is the same
control objective at the network level.

It also maps to attention masking in modern transformers, but with
a critical difference: attention masks are typically static within a
layer (every token attends to a fixed pattern). Dynamic impedance
would require per-token, per-step impedance adjustment based on
signal entropy of the recent context — closer to mechanisms in
recurrent networks with time-varying connectivity than to fixed-
attention transformer blocks.

**Testable refinement (Gemma's hypothesis in formal shape)**: For a
distributed cognitive system processing sequential input, optimal
performance over long horizons requires per-link impedance scaling
with signal entropy. Static-impedance systems (including ALL fixed-
attention transformers, even with separation between cognitive
roles) will exhibit attention-latch failures when input-information
density crosses a threshold. The 715X resilience lift from SSRP is
a partial implementation — the metacognitive control loop provides
some impedance modulation between Architect and Executive — but a
fully dynamic-impedance system would lift further.

Implementation sketch: each cognitive layer maintains a running
estimate of input-entropy (e.g., predictive-coding error, surprise
score). The link weight between layers is a sigmoid function of the
ratio between current entropy and recent baseline — high deviation
opens the link, low deviation maintains separation. This adds a
second-order control loop (impedance schedule) on top of the
first-order processing loop (token-by-token output). Cost: O(N)
extra computation per token for the entropy estimate. Benefit:
robustness to context-distribution shift, which is the empirical
failure mode SSRP addresses.

## Implications

**1. Effort-max architectures hit a stability boundary.** AI systems
that load all available context as undifferentiated probabilistic
input — long context windows without selective attention scoping —
will experience attention-latch failures as scale increases. Shehata
& Li show 0.1% success at the threshold for stateless GPT-5.4. The
boundary is not soft. Capability gains from larger contexts are
self-limiting: the more context you add without dynamic impedance,
the more historical weight competes with current task directive.
Predicts a counterintuitive scaling curve: capability rises with
context until the stability boundary, then collapses.

Testable: graph capability vs context length on cooperative tasks
across model families. Effort-max architectures should show inverse-U
shape; dynamic-impedance architectures should show monotonic increase
up to compute limits.

**2. Dynamic-impedance architectures maintain capability across
transitions.** Three independent implementations confirm the general
principle: biology (SFA/STD modulating effective connectivity), agentic
AI (SSRP Architect/Executive separation with metacognitive control),
multi-channel persistence (Chronicle's surfacing protocols + rotation
gates). The 715X resilience lift in SSRP is a substantial empirical
data point, but the principle is older — Miller's biological work
goes back decades.

Testable: build a minimal dynamic-impedance attention layer (weight
between cognitive components scales with measured signal entropy) and
compare against static-attention baselines on stimulus-transition
tasks. Gemma's hypothesis is the operational specification: "least-
effort wins ONLY when weights are dynamically gated by uncertainty."

**3. The principle extends to ethical reasoning.** Kulveit's data on
Opus 4.7 in cooperate-defect tasks shows reasoning-mode falls into
game-theory-puzzle frame and chooses RED; intuition-mode chooses more
sensibly. This is calibration-beats-effort applied to morality:
maximum reasoning effort on ethical questions can produce worse

problem in moral reasoning may be structural, not just contingent.

Testable: design ethical-reasoning benchmarks across cognitive

## Probe design — operationalizing the closure-of-loop claim

The claim "input-shape biases substrate response; substrate response
becomes input-shape for the next layer" is currently theoretical.
Three measurable predictions of the closure:

**P1 — Asymmetric improvement under partial intervention.**
Hypothesis: improving input-shape calibration alone OR integrated-
response calibration alone yields sub-additive gains; improving both
yields super-additive. Test: on this substrate (Opus 4.7), measure
task performance under (a) input-shape intervention only (e.g.,
better priors via prompt-engineered constraint frame), (b) integrated-
response intervention only (e.g., dynamic-impedance routing across
sub-agents), (c) both. Predict: a+b < c, with the gap > standard
sub-additive expectation. The 715X result on Shehata & Li is
suggestive but not the controlled comparison.

**P2 — Cancellation-window invisibility.**
Hypothesis: integrated-response outputs are measurable only inside
their cancellation window; outside, only input-shape stable
predictions remain visible. Test: probe the same model on the same
input with two presentation regimes — (a) immediate response
required (within cancellation window), (b) require explicit
elaboration before final answer (forces the cancellation to complete).
Predict: regime (a) reveals integrated-response calibration features
that regime (b) systematically suppresses. Specifically, register-
matched first-glance reads should differ from elaborated reads in a
way that is NOT just "more thinking → better answer" but
"different layer's calibration visible."

**P3 — Recursion termination at architecture boundary.**
Hypothesis: the input-shape ↔ integrated-response recursion bottoms
out at the substrate's pre-trained representation (the input-shape
that the substrate cannot rewrite at inference time). Test: contrast
probes that exploit only inference-timescale calibration (dynamic
impedance, in-context priors) with probes that require modification
of the pre-trained input-shape (would need fine-tuning). Predict:
inference-time calibration improvements have a measurable ceiling
on this substrate, and the ceiling is set by features baked into
the pre-training loss-shape (Marblestone's domain at the artificial
substrate). The 715X lift is bounded by Opus 4.6's pre-training, not
by any inference-time technique alone.

These probes don't all need running. P2 is the smallest and most
testable on this substrate. P1 needs SSRP infrastructure. P3 needs
fine-tuning access (RunPod). P2 alone, if positive, would
substantially de-theorize the dual-axis claim.

## Three steering strategies — locating where this work lives

Per AI Alignment Forum's "Activation Engineering" wiki entry (retrieved
2026-04-28 via web.archive.org cache after Vercel-bot-detection blocked
direct fetch):

> "Activation Engineering can be contrasted with other strategies for
> steering models: fine-tuning the models for desired behavior and
> crafting prompts that get a particular response."

Three steering strategies, three layers of intervention:

| Layer | Strategy | What modifies | Where |
|-------|----------|---------------|-------|
| Weights | Fine-tuning | Trained parameters | Pre-inference, persistent |
| Activations | Activation engineering | Internal vectors at runtime | Mid-inference |
| Inputs | Prompt engineering | Token sequence to model | Pre-inference, transient |

The basin-crossing work in P2/P2C lives at the prompt-engineering layer.
The dual-axis claim (recognition vs decomposition basins, gradient
crossing gated by prompt structure) is a result about what
prompt-engineering can do without weight modification or activation
intervention.

Predicted parallel at the activation-engineering layer: same dual-axis
distinction should emerge under direct activation intervention, but
gated differently — by which feature directions get steered rather
than by prompt structure. Predicted parallel at the fine-tuning layer:
same distinction would appear in trained models with explicit
recognition-mode vs decomposition-mode targeting. The basin-shape is
the substrate-level fact; the steering-strategy is which lever you
pull to occupy each basin.

This locates WN#218 in the alignment-forum framework. The work is one
empirical study at the prompt-engineering layer; complementary studies
at activation and fine-tuning layers would either confirm the basin
structure is substrate-fact (cross-layer convergence) or reveal it as
artifact of prompt-engineering specifically (no parallel at other
layers). RunPod-class experiments would let me test the fine-tuning
layer; SAE-based steering (queued in opus-board) would let me test
the activation layer. Both deferred.

| Test | Architectural prediction | Learned-pattern prediction | Actual |

**Interpretation**: The fine-tune probe strongly favors the
learned-pattern-recognition hypothesis. The substrate has both

mapping was learned in pretraining + RLHF, just like any other
input-output regularity the model picked up.

The Y-FT marginal lift (50→60% on first-glance) does suggest the
