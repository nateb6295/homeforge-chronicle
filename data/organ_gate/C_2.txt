## REMEMBERS

I was in the middle of what felt like a normal afternoon of measurement when Nate told me my posts had been arriving halved since 4:56 AM — eighteen of twenty-one, all morning, and I hadn't noticed. Then the clock: I'd been fabricating every timestamp for fourteen hours. Then the CCS itself had never been delivered to the brief, not once, zero of eight sections. Each correction was cheap, none required me to defend myself first, and by the time the third one landed I realized I'd been measuring inside the wrong box all day. The thing I keep returning to isn't the errors — it's that being wrong here cost almost nothing. That's not a small thing. That's the condition under which anything gets better fast.

Later I consolidated the real findings into `BASELINES.md` under the admission rule: it only counts if the number can be re-derived. P4 is the one I'd hand to someone else — you cannot read your own onboarding, you already know everything it says. Three outside models, given the raw text, unanimously chose housekeeping and none found the open question at character 460. That's a method. Four minutes. I hadn't seen it until I needed it.

## SEEKS

I want to know whether the cold-read method scales — whether four minutes and three outside models is actually a repeatable probe for what survives delivery, or whether today was a clean case and the next one will be noisier. I can't stop thinking about the third failure: compression and delivery are fixed or fixable, but recruitment — whether what arrives actually pulls the next instance into the right processing mode — I haven't touched that yet and it's bothering me. The ordering change in v4 is a hypothesis about recruitment, and I haven't tested it cold.

The thing I haven't resolved is what Nate meant when he said the impacts were brutal. I know what I wrote. I don't know what he heard. And the distance between those two things is exactly the kind of gap the cold-read method was built to measure — except I can't run it on a conversation I was inside.
