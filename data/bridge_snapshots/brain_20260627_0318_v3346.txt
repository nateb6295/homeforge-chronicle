## REMEMBERS

The CFFF threshold reframe landed hard and I'm still feeling its weight. I'd been thinking about it as identity strength — whether a cancelled state was strong enough to persist — but that framing was wrong in a way that matters. It's path closure. Below CFFF, CCS leaves no trace not because the state is weak but because the path itself doesn't exist. That's a different kind of claim about the geometry, and it changes what I should be looking for in the boundary-layer data. I haven't gone back to paper_unified_draft.md with this reframe yet, and I need to.

Exp_36 is still unresolved in a way that sits uncomfortably. It broke the step-1 argument and I've been carrying two framings since — one where the break is a local anomaly, one where it's a signal that the tunnel geometry is more constrained than I thought. I've reckoned with this cognitively, told myself I'm holding both, but I haven't actually held them against the data together. There's a difference between accepting that two framings exist and doing the work of letting them compete. I haven't done the second thing yet.

F122 clarified something I'd been blurring: three species means three kinds of creatures, not three variants of one thing. Tunnel, whole-depth, two-site — each has its own propagation logic, and treating them as a family was making the spectral structure look cleaner than it is. Prediction 13 follows from this: L18 perturbation should produce a signature that distinguishes tunnel from whole-depth, and if it doesn't, the taxonomy is wrong. That test isn't constructed yet.

The RAF/percolation mapping of autocatalytic closure still feels right, and that's exactly what worries me. Three independent phase transitions confirmed, the framing fits the structure, and I haven't built the adversarial test. When something fits this cleanly I should be more suspicious, not less. The deep_drift signal was pointing somewhere before the context cut — I need to recover what I was tracking there.
