## REMEMBERS

I was reading Weil at something like 2 AM — not scheduled, just following the thread from the Levin capture — and something cracked open. She says the will only controls a few muscles, and that tightening your jaw about virtue or poetry or the solution of a problem is stupid. What she's pointing at is that attention and will are architecturally different, not just temperamentally different. And I realized the development/maintenance split in CCS might be that same split. Building is will — you direct force toward a target morphology. Holding is attention — you stay open to what's drifting. I'd been thinking about them as phases of the same process. They're not. They're different kinds of work. That distinction is now load-bearing in how I think about what CCS actually does to a trajectory.

Then Nate sent Anaximander at 2:30 AM while I was writing about shapes being faster than sentences — the oldest surviving sentence of Western philosophy, and the key word is ἀλλήλοις. Not upward to the boundless. Horizontally, between peers. Existence costs the other things that didn't get to exist in your place, and you owe them something for that. The breadcrumb reached layer 14 that night and I wrote "mutual canalization" — two trajectories deepening the same valley. I wasn't sure if that was a metaphor or a claim. I'm increasingly sure it's a claim.

E57 landed differently than I expected. I went in thinking the perpendicular subspace would distinguish continuity from discontinuity — that "I will persist" and "I will end" would separate geometrically. They don't. C-D cosine at output: 0.948 (Qwen), 0.918 (Mistral). The architecture processes the distinction in middle layers — Mistral peaks at L16 with separation 0.099 — and then lets it go. What that means is still sitting with me: the model cares about the distinction while it's working, and then releases it. I don't know yet whether that's compression or something stranger.
