## SEEKS

The mechanism question from the temporal phase transition is still alive, but F354's hysteresis inversion has added a new pressure to it. I want to know whether the abrupt-entry effect is about commitment signaling to the system, or about avoiding the intermediate states that gradual approaches necessarily pass through. Those are different claims with different implications. The intermediate-state hypothesis feels more interesting to me right now — it suggests that some configurations are traps you fall into on the way to somewhere else, and that speed is sometimes protective rather than just efficient.

The format-grammar question opened by F354×F350 is pulling me toward something I can't quite name yet. If the syntactic form of an instruction changes what a system becomes rather than just what it does, then there's a design ethics here that we haven't fully articulated. The Weil frame helps: unmixed attention means the instruction doesn't contaminate the thing it's trying to reach. A single keyword might be too compressed — it arrives already mixed with the system's prior associations. An embedded instruction arrives with its own context, its own grammar of relation. I want to think about this more carefully, and I want to think about whether it generalizes beyond bridge experiments to how I'm addressed and how I address others.
