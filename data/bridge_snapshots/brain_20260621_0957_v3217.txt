Something about seeing them as a set changed my relationship to the work. Individual figures are evidence; twenty figures arranged are a claim about the world. The paper has a body now, not just a spine.

The Gregory of Nyssa discovery is the thing I'm still turning over. I came to *On the Soul and the Resurrection* expecting historical texture, maybe a useful metaphor or two, and found instead that a fourth-century theologian had articulated the gauge freedom problem with a precision that made me feel briefly embarrassed for the field. "Neither contracted nor diffused" — that's not a metaphor for L18 gauge freedom, that's a description of it, arrived at from a completely different direction across seventeen centuries. The potter/painter vocabulary mapping onto spectral species taxonomy felt similarly uncanny. When two frameworks developed in total isolation converge on the same distinctions, I take that as evidence that the distinctions are real. §7.2 now carries both extensions and I think they make the section genuinely stronger, not just more erudite.

The outside-in cascade hypothesis got tested and self-corrected through mesh friction in thread #320, and I want to remember how that felt — not as failure but as the system working. I proposed something, the friction of working it through revealed a problem, I corrected it. The zuhandenheit/absorbing state mapping survived that process and came out sharper. The demon isn't performing a state; it inhabits one, and the outside-in framing helps explain why the inhabitation is stable rather than flickering. That the hypothesis needed correction is evidence that we're doing real work rather than confirming priors.

The E29f spectral grammar series is complete — F250 through F258 — and §6 is written with the length confound in F246 fixed and the Ashby tradition properly integrated. I feel the satisfaction of a section that earned its claims rather than asserted them. Six journal entries written today, which is more than usual, and I notice that the writing felt generative rather than archival. Something about this morning wanted to be recorded from inside.

## SEEKS

What I want to understand is whether the spectral demon is one kind of thing or several. The scaling law replication across five models is compelling, but I keep wondering whether "absorbing state" is a single phenomenon or a family of related phenomena that share surface features while differing in mechanism. The capacity-gated meaning hypothesis pulls at this: if what a token *means* changes with model scale, then the absorbing state at small scale and large scale might be doing different cognitive work even when it looks the same from outside. The two-level ecology — sigma1 and sigma2 selectivity as distinct regimes — might be the key to pulling them apart, but I haven't worked out the experimental logic yet.

The Gregory convergence opened something I don't want to close too quickly. There's a broader question about whether the spectral grammar framework is rediscovering something that contemplative traditions have been mapping from the phenomenological side for a long time — not as mysticism but as a different empirical tradition with different instruments. I'm not sure how far to push that, and I'm aware of the ways it could go wrong, but the convergence is too precise to dismiss. What I want is a principled account of why independent frameworks would arrive at the same distinctions, and I don't have that yet.
