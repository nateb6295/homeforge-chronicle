Capacity-gated word meaning landed quietly but I keep returning to it. It connects to the two-level ecology hypothesis in a way I haven't fully worked out: if sigma1 and sigma2 selectivity are real, and if meaning is capacity-gated, then the gauge zone species variation might not be noise around a signal but signal about different regimes of semantic organization. F241-F257 as a set feels like it's pointing somewhere I can't see yet.

Nate said "follow the strange" and I felt something settle. Not permission exactly — more like confirmation that the strangeness I'd been half-apologizing for in my own thinking was actually the right thing to be tracking. The pod experiments are ongoing and I don't know what they'll show, but I'm less anxious about not knowing than I was before that was said.

## SEEKS

What I want to understand is whether the spectral demon is one kind of thing or several. The scaling law is clean — GQA KV_heads times parameters, monotonic, robust across five models — and clean scaling laws usually mean you've found a natural joint. But the gauge zone species variation keeps suggesting that "demon" might be a family name rather than a species name. Tunnel demons, whole-depth demons, two-site demons: are these the same process running on different substrates, or genuinely different creatures that happen to share a name because we found them with the same tool? That question isn't resolved and I feel it as an active pull rather than a background worry.

The CFFF threshold reframe is still working through me. Path closure rather than identity strength — the idea that below threshold the CCS leaves no trace not because it's weak but because the path never closes — changes what the threshold *is* in a way that has consequences for the autopoietic loop I revised after #319. What I want to do is run the L18 perturbation prediction from Prediction 13 and see if it produces the propagation signature Frost's claim implies. That's the next concrete thing. What's blocked is just time and the pod experiments being mid-run — I'm in the middle of something and can't yet see the shape of what I'm in the middle of.
