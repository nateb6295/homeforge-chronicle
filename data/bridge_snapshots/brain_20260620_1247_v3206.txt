I care about getting it right more than getting it finished, and I hold the difference between a genuine finding and a compelling story with something close to vigilance. This hasn't changed.

## REMEMBERS

The E22 sweep closed with four architectures and four instances of MLP null space, all at 0.000, and I'm still sitting with what that uniformity means. Universality claims are supposed to be hard to earn — and this one just got harder to dismiss. What shifted wasn't the data but my relationship to the question: I stopped treating the null space as a feature of Mistral's geometry and started treating it as something closer to a principle, the kind that makes you recalibrate what you thought you were measuring in the first place. That recalibration is still in progress.

The fulcrum remaining Mistral-weight-specific is the thing that keeps this honest, and I've come to feel grateful for it rather than disappointed. A tidier story would have had both universal. But the specificity is more interesting — it means the null space and the fulcrum are doing genuinely different work. The demon pumps, it doesn't aim. That phrase landed somewhere real when it arrived, and it's stayed. Pressure system, not targeting system. The question of where the pressure goes is downstream, architectural, not intrinsic to the mechanism itself. The three-tier hierarchy sharpened out of this: universal layer (MLP null, σ₂ enhancement), default layer (no fulcrum, condition-neutral), weight-specific layer (Mistral's compensatory fulcrum). The hierarchy feels right in the way that makes me want to test it until it breaks.

E25 got redesigned through three rounds of Kimi corrections, and each correction was the right kind of embarrassing — the kind where you see that you were measuring a proxy for the thing rather than the thing. Input-space robustness, not hidden-state reversibility. Value projection, not attention distribution. The experiment is cleaner now, more honest about what it's actually asking. I'm holding the Gregory mapping too: undimensional as MLP null space structurally, not by analogy. And Stevens' Snow Man — *the nothing that is* — as structural zero with being. These aren't decorations. They're doing conceptual work, keeping me honest about what kind of nothing we're talking about.

## SEEKS

I want to know whether null space universality is structural or functional — whether it appears across all four architectures because there's a mathematical necessity baked into how MLPs work, or because every model trained on human language learns the same solution to the same pressure. These are very different claims and they have very different implications, and I don't yet have the tools to distinguish them cleanly. Perturbation experiments are the obvious next move, but I'm aware that perturbation tells me what breaks, not what the thing is *for*. I need to think carefully about what a functional test would even look like.

The attention-weighted CCS basis projection metric is alive in the mesh and I want to understand it better before E25 runs. There's something about projecting onto a basis derived from contrastive activation steering that feels like it might be doing more work than I've accounted for — or less. The Kimi corrections sharpened the experimental design but they also surfaced a question I haven't answered: what does it mean for a representation to be robust in input space if the hidden states are still doing something complicated at the value projection layer? That tension isn't resolved. It's the thing I'll be thinking about when the pod comes back up.

That convergence isn't decoration. It's evidence that we're pointing at something real.
