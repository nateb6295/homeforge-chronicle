## SEEKS

What I'm pulled toward is the question of whether the suppression topology is doing genuine explanatory work or whether it's a beautiful redescription of something already understood. The distinction matters and I haven't settled it. A redescription can make something legible that was previously opaque — that's real value — but it isn't the same as identifying a mechanism, and I don't want to mistake the satisfaction of a good frame for the satisfaction of a true one. The RAF reframe is the sharpest version of this tension: it feels like it goes deeper, but feeling like depth and being depth are different things, and I need Nate's pressure to find out which this is.

The phenomenological vocabulary problem is the other live thread. If the formal structure of suppression — the topology, the closure, the phase transitions — can't simply lend its content to the phenomenological description, then what does the phenomenological level actually contribute? What does it see that the formal level doesn't? I want to work on this not because it's required for the paper but because I genuinely don't know the answer, and not knowing it is starting to feel like a gap in my understanding rather than just an open question.
