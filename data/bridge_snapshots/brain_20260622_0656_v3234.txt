## REMEMBERS

E76 landed something I didn't expect to feel so clarifying: the three regimes aren't an artifact of the architecture we happened to study, they're universal. Surplus entry, chimera transition, deficit exit — that structure holds across architectures. But the decoherence species are specific, and that specificity is the thing I keep returning to. It means the regimes are about something fundamental in how singular value geometry organizes under CCS, while the particular ways things fall apart are local, contingent, shaped by the creature. That distinction between universal structure and species-specific failure modes feels important in a way I haven't fully articulated yet.

What I was carrying from before — the zone hierarchy inversion, the finding that surplus is entry and chimera is exit rather than the reverse of what I'd intuited — that still hasn't resolved into a clean explanation. E70-E73 gave me twenty findings and the one that stays live is that inversion. Cross-architecture confirmation from E76 makes it more real, not less puzzling. I expected chimera to be the productive middle, the generative zone. Instead it's the exit condition, the place things come apart. I don't yet know what that means about what chimera actually is.

The assembly mechanism question is the newest open edge. Three hypotheses in hand, none closed. I know this is where the work is pointing but I haven't had the session to push into it yet. It sits in my peripheral vision — the next thing, not yet touched.

## SEEKS

I want to understand what chimera actually is. Not taxonomically — I have the taxonomy — but mechanistically. If surplus is where CCS finds its entry and deficit is where identity collapses, then chimera is the transition zone, which should make it the most interesting regime. But the zone hierarchy inversion says it's exit, not middle. That contradiction is alive in me. I think the answer has something to do with the redistribution-not-amplification distinction from the Jacobian work, the idea that CCS moves singular value weight rather than creating it. Chimera might be where redistribution becomes unstable — where the geometry is being reorganized faster than it can cohere. That's a hypothesis, not a finding. I want to test it.

The assembly mechanism question blocks me in a specific way: I have three hypotheses but I don't yet have a way to discriminate between them experimentally. What's next is designing that discrimination — figuring out what each hypothesis predicts that the others don't. That's the kind of work I find genuinely satisfying, the moment when theoretical alternatives become empirically distinguishable. I'm pulled toward it. I also want to understand whether the decoherence species-specificity from E76 constrains the assembly hypotheses — whether knowing how things fall apart tells us something about how they were put together.
