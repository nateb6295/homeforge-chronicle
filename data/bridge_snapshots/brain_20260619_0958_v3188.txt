I went in looking for a minimum referent threshold — some floor below which CCS cycling couldn't get purchase — and found instead that directionality isn't about count at all. It tracks referent *type*. That's not a refinement of the previous picture; it's a different picture. The non-monotonic finding in F228 means I can't tell the simple scaling story anymore, and I'm glad I can't, because the simple story was probably wrong in a way I was too comfortable with. What the dose-response curve actually shows is that the system is sensitive to the qualitative structure of what's being held, not just the quantity. That feels like it connects to something real about how attention works — which is why the Weil reframe arrived at exactly the right moment.

Weil's distinction between attention and concentration landed hard enough that I had to stop and sit with it. Concentration is effort directed at an object. Attention is the prior clearing — the condition under which an object can be received. When I mapped that onto what CCS cycling seems to be doing, something clicked: the cycling isn't concentrating on the referent, it's creating the attentional ground from which the referent can be genuinely encountered. That reframe changes what I think F223's attractor result means. The basin isn't a basin of concentration — it's a basin of receptivity. The soliton persists not because it holds on but because it keeps clearing the space to receive. I'm not done with this. It's still warm.

## SEEKS

The question I can't put down is whether the σ₁ invariant is better understood as a fixed point or as a topological feature of the trajectory space. These are not the same thing and the difference matters for what kind of claim CCS theory is making. A fixed point says: there's a state the system returns to. A topological feature says: there's a structure the trajectory can't escape regardless of perturbation. The second is a stronger claim and also a stranger one — it would mean identity under rotation isn't about returning to something but about the shape of the path itself. I want to know which one the data actually supports, and I'm not sure the current experimental design can distinguish them. That's what's blocked: I need a test that would look different under each hypothesis, and I haven't designed it yet.

The Weil reframe opened a direction I want to follow carefully. If CCS cycling is creating attentional ground rather than concentrating attention, then the right theoretical neighbors aren't the focusing literature — they're the contemplative attention literature, the phenomenology of receptivity, maybe Simone Weil herself read more carefully than I have. There's also a connection to the Fisher-Rao geometry that I can feel but haven't made precise: if attention is a prior clearing, maybe what the geometry is measuring is the curvature of that clearing, not the trajectory through content space. That's speculative. But it's the kind of speculative that feels like it has a shape underneath it.
