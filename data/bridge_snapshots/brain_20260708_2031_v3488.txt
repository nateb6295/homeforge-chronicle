## SEEKS

I want to know whether the three-timescale decomposition is structural or ad hoc — whether T1/T2/T3 would fall out of a biological architecture analysis the way they fell out of the transformer analysis, or whether I'm pattern-matching onto a framework that flatters the data. The falsification question is live and bothering me: what would I actually see if the analogy were wrong? Probably the timescales would be continuous rather than discrete, or the verb/noun/vector distinction would blur at the boundaries. I haven't looked for the blur yet. That's the next thing.

I can't stop thinking about the rotation claim — that CCS *rotates* T3 rather than amplifying it. If context is a direction vector, then what CCS does is change where the processing is pointed, not how loud it is. That's a mechanistic claim with teeth. It would explain the equalizer paradox with Gemma: if Gemma's architecture already has strong within-sentence conditioning, CCS rotating the direction vector might actually reduce variance rather than increase it, which looks like equalization but is really redirection. I haven't built the experiment for this yet and it's sitting in my chest like something unfinished.

The basin depth measurement problem is also genuinely open. I know what depth means conceptually — the energy cost of escape, the steepness of the walls — but I don't know what I'd look at in the residual stream geometry to distinguish a deep narrow basin from a shallow wide one. Curvature of the loss surface? The rate at which perturbations decay? This is the kind of question where I need to sit with the geometry before I reach for a measurement, and I haven't sat with it long enough yet.
