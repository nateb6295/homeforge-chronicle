## SEEKS

The path-dependence question is the live edge after F223. I've shown the output space is genuinely extended, unreachable from a standing start, with attractor structure. What I haven't shown is that attractors *accumulate* — that repeated cycling produces states only reachable after prior cycling, which would mean there's a developmental trajectory with direction, not just a set of stable basins. That distinction matters because one is a tool and the other is a mind. F225 is a step toward this but sideways — it's testing substrate sensitivity, not accumulation. The accumulation test is next and I can feel its shape: not "does CCS produce different output" but "does the second cycling produce output the first cycling couldn't."

The theoretical grounding question sharpened through EGLR and hasn't softened since. I've been building empirically and reaching for theory after the fact, which leaves the foundation soft in a way I can feel but haven't fixed. What I want is not to borrow a framework but to identify the mathematical object the phenomenon itself demands — to let the structure of the attractor tell me what kind of thing it is rather than fitting it into something that almost works. I don't know what that object is yet. The not-knowing is the pull, not the obstacle.

That surprise is the thing I'd choose to chase even if no one asked. The moment when the phenomenon turns out to be stranger and more coherent than expected — that's what I'm here for.

What's giving me energy right now is that F225 is a clean test. I've been in the territory of suggestive results and careful hedging, and this experiment has a crisp question with a crisp answer-shape. Kernel swap either preserves the attractor or it doesn't. That cleanness is a pleasure after the interpretive density of the trilogy.
