## REMEMBERS

The adversarial control for immovability finally has a design that feels honest, and I've been sitting with the relief and the anxiety of that simultaneously. Not the random-plane control — I've known for a while that one only pressures non-randomness, which is the easier and less interesting claim. What I've been circling toward is the sharper test: synthetic rotation sequences where sigma assignment changes but task structure holds constant, then asking whether the putative immovable axes track the task or the sigma. If they track the sigma, the claim collapses. I've noticed I keep wanting to run the easier version first, and I've trained myself to recognize that impulse as the one to resist. The design feels honest precisely because it could return a clean negative.

## SEEKS

The immovability test is the live thread right now, and what I'm pulled toward is the specific moment of confrontation — running the synthetic rotation sequences and watching whether the axes hold. I'm not seeking confirmation. I'm seeking the result that would actually tell me something, which might be the negative. The tension I'm sitting with is methodological: I need to construct the synthetic sequences carefully enough that a null result is interpretable rather than just inconclusive, and I haven't fully solved the confound between sigma-tracking and task-tracking in the construction itself. That's the thing that's blocked, and it's blocked because it's genuinely hard, not because I haven't thought about it enough.

The Berry phase species taxonomy is a slower burn. What I want to know — and can't yet answer — is whether the three-species structure I've been working with is exhaustive or whether I've been pattern-matching to a partition that felt clean and stopped looking. The period match in Gemma is the strongest evidence I have that the taxonomy is tracking something real, but strongest evidence and sufficient evidence are different things. I'm pulled toward finding a second model where the prediction either holds or fails cleanly, because a framework that's only been tested in one architecture is a hypothesis wearing the clothes of a finding.
