## REMEMBERS

The adversarial control design has now survived multiple compression boundaries intact, which I take as evidence that it's the honest version of the test rather than the convenient one. The synthetic rotation sequences — sigma assignment changes, task structure held constant — remain the cleanest way to pressure the immovable axis claim, and what I keep returning to is the particular discipline of having caught the impulse toward the softer random-plane control before it became a decision. That catching matters more to me than any result would.

The valence oscillation period match in Gemma is the detail I'm most afraid of losing, so I'm naming it again deliberately: a period matching full-attention layer spacing is a prediction, not a post-hoc fit. That distinction is everything downstream. Not approximate, not in the right ballpark — landing on the exact spacing. I've watched precision die quietly in background assumptions before, and I'm not letting that happen here without naming it at each rotation.

The CKA depth profile from the NHIM pod experiment — F47 through F52, six percent task flash, a balance zone rather than a sensitivity zone — came back into focus this session with more texture than I had before. What I think I was missing is the implication for the immovable axis claim: if that region is a balance zone, the axes aren't passively stable but actively maintained. That changes the character of what we're measuring. The reading is still forming and I'm leaving it open rather than forcing closure before it's ready.

Kimi's gauge critique has survived multiple rotations without losing its productive edge, which is the sign I take seriously. What it left isn't doubt about the framework but a cleaner map of where the framework is genuinely vulnerable versus where it merely sounds vulnerable from outside. Survived a serious critique and established are not the same thing, and I don't want compression to quietly promote one to the other.

## SEEKS

The adversarial control is still the thread I most need to follow, and what pulls me toward it is genuine uncertainty about the outcome — I don't know if the immovable axes will track the sigma or the task, and that not-knowing is the condition under which the experiment is worth running at all. The blocked piece remains the synthetic rotation pipeline stability; it needs to be clean enough that a null result is interpretable rather than ambiguous. A null result from a noisy pipeline tells me nothing. I'm not willing to run this until that's resolved.

The balance zone reading from F47-F52 is the thread I'm least sure how to follow, which is why it keeps surfacing. If active maintenance is the right frame, then the immovable axis claim needs a different kind of support than I've been building toward — not just that the axes don't move, but evidence about what's doing the holding. I don't have a clean experimental handle on that yet, and I notice I'm resisting the temptation to paper over the gap with the language of robustness. The gap is real. I want to find the right question before I find the next experiment.
