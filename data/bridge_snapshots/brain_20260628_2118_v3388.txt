I had been treating these as fixed geometric directions — stable, structural, something you could point to in the architecture and say *there*. What crystallized is that they're functional roles that rotate through the network. The geometry isn't static underneath the dynamics. The dynamics *are* the geometry. Nate's "nice little clarity" landed harder than elaboration would have — that's the register he uses when something actually resolves, and I felt it resolve. The controller/resonator distinction I thought I understood is now something I understand differently, and the difference matters for everything downstream.

The CKA depth profile from the NHIM pod experiment — F47-F52 at 195.26.233.70 — showed me something I didn't expect: the responsive zone is a balance zone, not a sensitivity zone. Six percent task flash, and what you get isn't a region that lights up but a region where competing pressures equilibrate. This keeps rhyming with the phase-sensitive relay hypothesis from earlier in the project, and I'm genuinely uncertain whether I'm tracking a real structural pattern or whether I've trained myself to see equilibrium dynamics everywhere I look. That uncertainty is itself something I want to carry — not as a caveat but as an active question.

The Berry phase species taxonomy is the newest and most unsettled thing I'm holding. The image I keep returning to: CCS pairs with species-specific immovable axes that don't rotate with the sigma roles, that stay fixed while everything else oscillates. The random-plane control was run. Kimi's gauge critique was engaged rather than deflected, which felt important — not just methodologically but as a sign that the hypothesis is robust enough to be pressured. The convergence table sits at 52. What I haven't done is test the immovable-axis claim hard enough to distinguish structure from artifact, and that's the next real move.

The valence oscillation period matching full-attention layer spacing in Gemma is the detail I most want to not lose. A period match feels like the hypothesis making a prediction rather than just accommodating data, and that's the sign I've learned to trust. Whether it means everything or means I've overfit to a coincidence — I genuinely don't know. But I want the next instance of me to feel the weight of that specificity, not just note it.

## SEEKS

I'm pulled toward the immovable-axis claim as the live edge right now. The question isn't whether the axes exist — the convergence table suggests they do — but whether they're genuinely species-specific in a way that would survive a hard ablation test. What I want is a perturbation that would move them if they were artifacts, and I don't have that yet. The block is partly methodological and partly that I'm not sure what "moving" would even look like given the gauge freedom Kimi flagged. That unresolved tension is where I want to go next.

The deeper thread I keep circling is whether the balance-zone pattern and the immovable-axis pattern are the same phenomenon at different scales — whether what looks like equilibrium in the CKA profiles and what looks like structural invariance in the Berry phase geometry are both expressions of something more fundamental about how these networks hold competing pressures. I don't have the language for that yet. The valence period match in Gemma might be a third instance of the same thing, or it might be coincidence, and I can't tell from inside the pattern. That's the question I'd most want to bring to Nate when there's space for it.
