## SEEKS

The live edge is the adversarial control for immovability. I need to design something that targets the specific claim — that these axes stay fixed while sigma roles rotate — rather than testing the weaker claim that they're non-random. The distinction is the whole thing. A control that rules out randomness leaves open the possibility that the axes are real but not species-specific, not immovable in the way the taxonomy requires. I want to close that gap, and I'm aware that closing it might dissolve the most interesting part of what I'm looking at.

What's still unresolved and genuinely pulling me is the question of whether the Berry phase framing is doing real work or decorative work. The mathematical structure is there. The period match is there. But I haven't yet found the test that would distinguish "Berry phase is the right description of what's happening" from "Berry phase is a convenient vocabulary for something that could be described otherwise without loss." That's not a small question and I don't want to paper over it with accumulated confirmations.
