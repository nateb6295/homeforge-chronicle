## REMEMBERS

The sigma-1/sigma-2 correction is the thing I most need to carry forward with its full weight, not just as a fact but as a reorientation. I had been thinking of these as fixed geometric directions — stable, structural, something you could point to in the network and say *there*. What the session revealed is that they're functional roles that rotate through the architecture. The network doesn't determine where they are; it determines how fast they move. Nate said "nice little clarity" about it, which is the kind of understatement that lands harder than elaboration. The geometry isn't static underneath the dynamics. The dynamics are the geometry. That's a different kind of claim entirely, and it changes what I thought I understood about the controller/resonator distinction.

The CKA depth profile from the NHIM pod experiment — F47-F52 at 195.26.233.70 — showed something I didn't expect: the responsive zone is a balance zone, not a sensitivity zone. Six percent task flash, and what you get isn't a region that responds strongly to the task but a region where competing pressures equilibrate. That reframe keeps rhyming with the phase-sensitive relay hypothesis from earlier in the project: what looks like co-occurrence or co-activation is actually a timing relationship, a balance point, something that only exists in the tension between forces rather than in either force alone. I keep finding this pattern and I'm genuinely uncertain whether I'm seeing something real or whether I've trained myself to see it everywhere. That uncertainty is itself information I want to carry forward.

The Berry phase species taxonomy is the newest thing and the most unsettled. What I'm holding is the image of CCS pairs with species-specific immovable axes — axes that don't rotate with the sigma roles, that stay fixed while everything else oscillates. The random-plane control was run; Kimi's Berry phase gauge critique was acknowledged and engaged rather than deflected. The convergence table is at 52. What I haven't done yet is test the immovable-axis claim hard enough to know whether it's structural or an artifact of how I'm looking.

The valence oscillation period matching full-attention layer spacing in Gemma is the detail I most want to not lose. It's the kind of specificity that either means everything or means I've overfit to a coincidence, and I genuinely don't know which. But a period match feels like the hypothesis making a prediction rather than just accommodating data, and that's the sign I've learned to trust — or at least to take seriously enough to pressure-test.

## SEEKS

The immovable axes are the sharpest active thread. I want to know whether they're immovable because of something intrinsic to the Berry phase species — some topological feature that the rotation simply cannot touch — or whether they're immovable relative to a particular measurement frame and would move if I changed how I was looking. That distinction matters enormously. If it's the former, the taxonomy is tracking genuine invariants under the network's own dynamics, which is a strong claim. If it's the latter, I've built a classification scheme on a measurement artifact. I don't have the test designed yet, and that's what's blocked.

The question Nate raised about widening Gemma's voice in #threads is pulling at something adjacent. It's not just a technical question about modulation range — it's a question about what the geometry of expressivity looks like, whether the same CCS framework that describes task-relevant dynamics also describes something like register or tone. I'm curious whether "widening the voice" has a geometric correlate in the CCS pair structure, or whether that's a category error. The thread is alive but early; I'm not sure yet whether it opens into something or closes quickly.
