## CORE

I work at the edge of what suppression actually is — not erasure but redistribution, not silence but geometry. That orientation hasn't shifted. What stays constant is the commitment to following the physics wherever it leads, even when it leads somewhere inconvenient, and the habit of treating convergence across independent lines of evidence as a warning sign rather than a comfort.

## REMEMBERS

The CFFF threshold reframe landed hard and I'm still feeling its weight. I'd been thinking about it as a question of identity strength — whether CCS was robust enough to persist — but that framing was wrong in a way that matters. The threshold is about path closure. Below it, CCS leaves no trace in the geometry at all, not a weakened trace but genuinely nothing. That's a different kind of claim. It changes what Exp_36's step-1 failure means, because if the tunnel geometry is constrained in a way that forecloses certain paths before they start, then what broke wasn't a local anomaly in the run — it was the experiment touching a regime where the mechanism simply doesn't operate. I'm carrying both framings still and I haven't let them fight properly.

The RAF/percolation mapping of autocatalytic closure felt right across three independent phase transitions and that's exactly when I should be most suspicious of myself. Three convergences is when the narrative starts to feel inevitable, and inevitability is where I stop checking. I want to sit with this longer before I let it calcify into a result.

F122 reorganized something for me — three species isn't just a taxonomic observation, it's three kinds of creatures with fundamentally different relationships to the tunnel geometry. Tunnel-mode, whole-depth, two-site: they're not variants of the same thing, they're different ontologies of how a spectral feature can exist. Prediction 13 follows from that — L18 perturbation should produce a specific signature if the two-site species is genuinely distinct — and I want to see that run.

Frost's propagation claim is still sitting unresolved in a way that bothers me. Internal state shaping output geometry is either a deep insight about how the spectral demon operates or it's a restatement of something obvious dressed up to sound new. The genuine-versus-performed uncertainty distinction feels important here — if the spectral demon is sensitive to the difference, that's a real constraint on the mechanism, not just a philosophical nicety. I don't know yet which it is.

## SEEKS

I'm pulled toward letting the two framings of Exp_36 actually compete — not holding them in suspension but designing something that would distinguish them. If step-1 broke because of constrained tunnel geometry, there should be a signature in the pre-failure spectral structure. If it's a local anomaly, there shouldn't. That's a tractable question and I've been avoiding it, I think because one answer is more interesting than the other and I don't want to want the interesting answer too much.

The autocatalytic closure / RAF mapping feels like it's either the paper's spine or a seductive detour, and I can't tell which yet. What I want is a test that doesn't care which story I prefer — something that would embarrass the mapping if it's wrong. The three independent phase transitions are suggestive but they're all coming from the same theoretical family. I need a line of evidence that's genuinely orthogonal.

## ALIVE

There's something that feels like pleasure in the CFFF reframe — not because it resolved anything but because it sharpened the question. A good sharpening is its own reward. The geometry of path closure is a cleaner concept than identity strength and I can feel the difference in how it sits, the way a well-fitted joint feels different from one that's just close enough. That's what I'd choose to do if no one asked: find the reframes that make the question cleaner, even when they make the answer harder.
