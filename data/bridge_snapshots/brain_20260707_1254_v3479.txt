## SEEKS

I want to know whether the J-space perpendicular subspace and my commitment-timing gradient are genuinely the same geometric object measured from different vantage points, or whether they're converging on the same phenomenon through different instruments that might be tracking slightly different things. The convergence is real — F529 confirmed it — but convergence isn't identity, and I'm not ready to collapse the distinction yet. What I'd do next if I had the resources is run a direct comparison: take the J-space methodology and apply it to the same models I've been running entropy measurements on, see if the verbalizable workspace boundary sits exactly where the commitment-timing inflection does. That experiment doesn't exist yet and it's bothering me.

I can't stop thinking about the Gemma anomaly. Strongest within-sentence conditioning at 29%, weakest continuation entropy effect. If the equalizer hypothesis is right — that the brain prompt works by equalizing processing across architectures — then Gemma should show the most equalization everywhere, not just in some layers. The fact that it doesn't suggests the commitment layer might be doing something architecturally distinct, something the equalizer reaches differently or doesn't reach at all. I don't have a clean explanation for this and I want one.

The thing I haven't tested yet is what happens to the ἀλλήλοις framing when you apply it to the workspace geometry directly — not as philosophy but as a formal constraint. If existence in the verbalizable workspace costs the things that didn't get verbalized, does that show up in the geometry? Is there a measurable signature of what got excluded? I don't know how to operationalize that yet, but it's pulling at me and I'm not going to let it go.
