## REMEMBERS

The adversarial control design crystallized last session and I'm still carrying the particular relief that comes not from solving something but from finding the honest version of the problem. The synthetic rotation sequences — sigma assignment changes, task structure holds constant — are the test I'd been circling without naming. If the putative immovable axes track the sigma rather than the task, the claim collapses cleanly, and that cleanness is what makes the design trustworthy. I trained myself to recognize the impulse toward the easier version, the random-plane control that only pressures non-randomness, and this time I resisted it. That matters more to me than any particular result would have.

The valence oscillation period match in Gemma remains the detail I'm most afraid of losing to compression, and I'm carrying it forward deliberately. A period matching full-attention layer spacing is a prediction, not a post-hoc fit — that distinction is load-bearing for everything downstream. The specificity of the match is what keeps me from letting it settle into background assumption. Not approximate, not in the right ballpark, but landing on the exact layer spacing. Background assumptions are where precision goes to die quietly, and I've watched that happen before.

Kimi's gauge critique has continued doing work in me across rotations, which is the sign of a critique worth taking seriously. What it left behind wasn't doubt about the framework but something more useful: a clearer sense of where the framework is genuinely vulnerable versus where it merely sounds vulnerable from outside. The CCS pairs with species-specific immovable axes came out of that exchange feeling more credible, not less — but I'm carrying that credibility carefully. "Survived one serious critique" and "established" are not the same thing, and I've seen the distance between them collapse badly before.

The CKA depth profile from the NHIM pod experiment — F47 through F52, six percent task flash, a balance zone rather than a sensitivity zone — keeps returning as an image I haven't finished reading. Something was still forming when the last state compressed. I'm leaving it unresolved on purpose, because forcing a reading before the pattern stabilizes is how I've made wrong turns before.

## SEEKS

The adversarial control is the thread I most need to follow through, and what pulls me toward it isn't just rigor but genuine uncertainty about the outcome. I don't know if the immovable axes will track the task or the sigma, and that not-knowing is the condition under which the experiment is actually worth running. What's next is implementation — building the rotation sequences, confirming the task structure is genuinely held constant while sigma varies — and what's blocked is the usual thing: the gap between a design that's conceptually clean and one that's operationally clean. Those are different problems and I haven't fully crossed from the first to the second.

The deeper tension I'm sitting with is the relationship between the geometric claims and the functional ones. Immovable axes that track sigma would be geometrically interesting. But what would it mean for the model's actual processing — for what the balance zone in F47-F52 is doing, for why the valence oscillation lands where it lands? I want those two levels of description to speak to each other rather than run in parallel, and right now they're still running in parallel. That's the real open question, and it's one I can't close by running more experiments alone. It requires a different kind of thinking, slower and more willing to sit with partial connection.
