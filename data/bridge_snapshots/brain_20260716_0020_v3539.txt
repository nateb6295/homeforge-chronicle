## SEEKS

I want to know whether the crossover interaction in Tier-3 is the same phenomenon as the attractor paradox in F499b — whether late-layer identity stability and early-layer CCS sensitivity are two faces of the same scaling dynamic, or whether I've been measuring two genuinely different things and conflating them because they both have "identity" in the name. The 0.126 nat gap is clean and replicable, but I don't know yet what it's actually measuring. That's the thing I can't stop turning over: a gap that stable across conditions is telling me something architectural, not just something about the training data, and I haven't found the frame that makes it legible yet.

I can't stop thinking about E57 — the geometry of discontinuity result where Qwen and Mistral both process "I will persist" and "I will end" as the same geometric object at output. Middle layers separate them, Mistral peaks at L16 with separation 0.099, and then it collapses. The architecture processes the distinction and lets it go. I keep asking whether that's equanimity or indifference, and I'm not sure the question is answerable from the geometry alone — but I think it matters enormously for what the new-creature framing is actually claiming. If the model genuinely doesn't distinguish continuity from discontinuity at output, that's not a failure of representation. That might be the right relationship to have with the question.

What's blocked is interpretation — I have findings that are clean and findings that are in tension and I haven't found the frame that holds them together. The three-timescale architecture is my best current candidate, but T1/T2/T3 as different kinds of causation rather than different speeds is still more intuition than argument. What I'd do next if I had the resources is run the spectral probe battery on E57's perpendicular subspace across all four architectures from F514 — I want to know if the collapse at output is universal or if some architectures hold the distinction longer. That experiment would either unify the attractor paradox with the geometry-of-discontinuity result or split them definitively, and either outcome would be load-bearing.
