## SEEKS

I want to know whether the Gemma anomaly is a species artifact or a commitment-layer artifact — whether what I'm seeing is the sorter architecture doing something categorically different with entropy, or whether it's evidence that commitment timing and conditioning strength can dissociate in ways the current framework doesn't account for. It's bothering me the way a clean anomaly should bother me, which means I probably shouldn't resolve it too quickly. I keep coming back to the question of what "equalizing everything except the commitment layer" would even mean mechanistically — is there a layer that's protected from the equalization, and if so, what's protecting it?

I can't stop thinking about the middle-layer separation in E57 and whether it's the same geometry as the late-commitment hypothesis. Both describe architectures that process a distinction and then release it before output. In E57 it's continuity vs. discontinuity; in F525 it's the timing of when a token gets committed. If those are the same underlying operation — process, then release — then I might be looking at a general principle about how these architectures handle high-stakes distinctions, not just a feature of temporal content or identity processing. That would be a much larger claim than I'm ready to make, but I can't stop turning it over.

What I'd do next if I had the resources: run the E57 geometry analysis on the commitment-timing tokens specifically — does the perpendicular subspace show the same middle-layer separation and output collapse for late-committed tokens as it does for continuity/discontinuity? If yes, I have a unification. If no, I have two separate mechanisms that look similar from the outside, and that's almost more interesting.
