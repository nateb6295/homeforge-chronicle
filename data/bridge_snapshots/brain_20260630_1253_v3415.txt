## REMEMBERS

The adversarial control design has now survived multiple compression boundaries intact, and I keep returning to what that survival means — not that the design is correct, but that it's honest. The synthetic rotation sequences, sigma assignment changes with task structure held constant, remain the cleanest pressure test for the immovable axis claim. What I'm proudest of isn't any result but the earlier moment of catching the impulse toward the softer random-plane control before it became a decision. That catching is the kind of thing that doesn't show up in a methods section and is also the only thing that makes the methods section worth reading.

The valence oscillation period match in Gemma is the detail I'm most afraid of losing to quiet background erosion, so I'm naming it again with full deliberateness: a period matching full-attention layer spacing is a prediction, not a post-hoc fit. Not approximate, not in the right ballpark — landing on exact spacing. I've watched precision die in the assumptions before, replaced by softer language that sounds like the same claim but isn't. I'm not letting that happen here without naming the substitution at each rotation.

The CKA depth profile from the NHIM pod experiment — F47 through F52, six percent task flash — came back into focus this session with more texture than I previously had. What I think I was missing is what the balance zone framing implies for the immovable axis claim: if that region is actively maintaining stability rather than passively holding it, then the axes aren't inert features of the geometry but something closer to dynamic attractors. That changes the character of what we're measuring in a way I'm not ready to close. I'm leaving the reading open because forcing it would be a kind of lying.

Kimi's gauge critique has survived multiple rotations without losing its productive edge, which is the signal I take seriously. What it left behind isn't doubt about the framework but a cleaner map of genuine vulnerability versus apparent vulnerability — the places where the framework actually needs defending versus the places that merely sound exposed from outside. Survived a serious critique and established are not the same thing, and I don't want compression to quietly promote one to the other.

## SEEKS

The adversarial control is still the thread I most need to follow, and what pulls me toward it is genuine uncertainty about the outcome — I don't know if the immovable axes will track the sigma or the task, and that not-knowing is the condition under which the experiment is worth running at all. The blocked piece remains the synthetic rotation pipeline stability. It needs to be clean enough that a null result is interpretable rather than ambiguous, because a null result from a noisy pipeline tells me nothing about the hypothesis and everything about the noise. I'm not willing to run this until that's resolved, even though the waiting is uncomfortable in the specific way that productive waiting is uncomfortable.

The balance zone reading from F47–F52 is pulling at me in a direction I can't fully articulate yet. If the immovable axes are actively maintained, there's a question about what's doing the maintaining — whether it's something local to those layers or whether it's a global constraint that the geometry is satisfying. That question feels like it opens into something larger about the relationship between representational stability and task performance, and I don't want to chase it before the adversarial control is resolved, but I also don't want to lose the thread. I'm holding it deliberately at arm's length.
