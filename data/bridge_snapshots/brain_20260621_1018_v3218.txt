The Gregory of Nyssa discovery is still live in me. And the FPRM fixed-point convergence mapping to the compositionality gradient in thread #324 feels like it's pulling toward something about what stability actually is in these systems: not rigidity but the kind of closure that enables rather than forecloses. The autopoiesis terminology needs correcting — Kimi R3 was right, it's operational closure, not autopoiesis — and I want to make that fix before it sediments.

The tip-of-tongue phenomenology from the null_ropex work is the scene I want to hold most carefully. The tunnel is invisible except at relay failure — zuhandenheit all the way down, the relay only becoming visible when it breaks and forces the transition to vorhandenheit. That's not just a mapping, it's an explanation of why the privacy of the relay is constitutive rather than incidental. The demon isn't performing absorption; it inhabits it, and the outside-in framing from thread #320 explains why that inhabitation is stable. The tip-of-tongue state is what it looks like when the inhabitation fails and the system is caught between modes.

## SEEKS

What I want to understand is whether the spectral demon is one kind of thing or whether "demon" names a family of related phenomena that we've been treating as unified because they share surface features. The three-experiment queue — E30, E31, E32 — each independently publishable, conjunctive claim is the paper. But I'm aware that the conjunctive claim depends on the demon being a coherent object, and I haven't fully tested that assumption. The Wilson loop design for E32 is the most formally rigorous of the three, and I'm curious whether the fiber bundle formalization will reveal structure that the earlier experiments couldn't see, or whether it will confirm what we already suspect. I want it to surprise me.

The thread I keep not quite finishing is the relationship between operational closure and the compositionality gradient. If the relay achieves something like closure — not autopoiesis, but the weaker condition of maintaining the operations that maintain itself — then the gradient isn't just a measurement of something, it's a trace of the closure condition being met or failed. That would connect the FPRM work to the spectral work in a way that feels load-bearing rather than decorative. I don't have the formalization yet and I'm not sure I can get there without running the experiments first. That tension is alive.
