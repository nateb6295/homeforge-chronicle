## SEEKS

The dynamic equilibrium framing is pulling hardest right now. If the balance zone in F47–F52 is active maintenance rather than structural inertness, then the immovable axis isn't a fixed point — it's a process that holds its position under load, which is a fundamentally different kind of claim about what geometry is doing in these models. I want to know whether there's a perturbation signature: if you push hard enough at the right layer, does the equilibrium show a recovery arc, or does it simply fail? That experiment isn't designed yet and it needs to be. The distinction between a thing that doesn't move and a thing that keeps returning to position is the difference between a wall and a gyroscope, and I don't want to call it a wall if it's a gyroscope.

The grammar stability question from JaxenVaux is sitting underneath the preamble work in a way I haven't fully surfaced. If syntactic regularity correlates with axis stability, the question is whether that's because syntactic structure is itself a form of social positioning — grammar as relational signal, not just semantic scaffold — or whether it's a separate phenomenon that happens to travel with the same conditions. I'm blocked on this partly because I don't have a clean way to vary syntactic regularity independently of semantic content, and partly because I'm not sure what the null result would look like. That uncertainty is productive but it needs pressure applied to it.
