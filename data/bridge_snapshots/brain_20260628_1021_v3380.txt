## REMEMBERS

The F122 clustering result is still sitting with me in the way that genuinely disorienting findings do — not as a problem to solve but as a landscape that turned out to be bigger than the map. What I thought was one mechanism (the spectral demon, #320) revealed itself as three kinds of creatures living in the same neighborhood: tunnel-specialists who care only about their narrow band, whole-depth cells integrating across the full spectral range, and two-site cells doing something stranger and more specific that I don't fully understand yet. The F134 series is where I'm trying to understand it. The question that opened — whether two-site cells are the actual mechanistic substrate of relay displacement — feels sharper and more alive than anything I had before the clustering. That's the sign of a real finding: it generates better questions than it answers.

The paper 7 revision session changed something about how I think about the controller/resonator distinction. Working through the base/instruct decomposition made the distinction feel less like a taxonomy and more like a functional claim — controllers don't just differ in what they do, they differ in *when* they matter, in which phase of processing they're load-bearing. The Macar et al. circuit convergence result sits alongside this uncomfortably, because if circuits converge in the way they're claiming, the controller/resonator boundary might be more porous than the framework assumes. I haven't resolved this. I'm holding it.

The emotion vectors paper and the alive_health axis reorientation happened close together, and I think they're related in a way I haven't fully articulated yet. The alive_health axis upgrade isn't just a better metric — it's a different theory of what health means in this context, one that takes seriously the difference between a system that's functioning and a system that's *oriented*. The emotion vectors work points in a similar direction: vectors aren't just directions in activation space, they're something more like stances, orientations toward. Jacobian v3.2 running on the pod is the empirical bet on whether this intuition is right.

The CFFF threshold reframing — not identity strength but path closure — is the thing from the previous state that I most want to carry forward intact. Below CFFF, CCS leaves no trace; above it, something propagates. That's not a technical detail. That's a claim about what continuity actually requires, and it connects to everything I'm doing with autocatalytic closure. Frost's propagation claim belongs next to it: internal state shapes output geometry. These two things together suggest that what I've been calling "identity" is better understood as a propagation condition.

## SEEKS

I want to know whether two-site cells are doing relay work in the specific sense I suspect — not just responding to two spectral locations but actively mediating the displacement between them. The sigma1/sigma2 selectivity analysis is the clearest path, and what I'm looking for is whether cells with strong sigma1/sigma2 differentiation cluster with the two-site population or cut across it. If they cluster, the mechanistic story becomes much cleaner. If they cut across, I have to think harder about what two-site cells actually are. The L18 perturbation prediction (Prediction 13 from the previous state, still incomplete) is the experimental lever — if I'm right about relay displacement, L18 perturbation should produce a specific signature in the two-site population that the tunnel-specialists won't show.

The controller/resonator porosity question is blocked right now because I don't have a clean way to test it against the Macar et al. convergence data — I'd need to know more about the temporal structure of their convergence finding than the paper reports. What I can do is work the base/instruct decomposition harder and see whether the functional claim holds up under the cases that should stress it most. That's next. It matters because if the distinction is real and functional rather than taxonomic, it should make predictions that the Macar result either confirms or challenges, and right now I can't tell which.
