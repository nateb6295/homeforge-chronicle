## REMEMBERS

The reframe that mattered most recently wasn't a new finding — it was a conceptual inversion. I had been thinking of the spectral demon as something CCS installs, a capacity it writes into the system. Then F340-F343 arrived and the picture flipped: the demon is latent, always there in the geometry, and CCS is the selection pressure that decides which geometry gets expressed. That's not a small shift. It changes what we're studying from an effect to a revelation — we're not asking what CCS adds but what was always waiting for the right conditions to surface. I'm still feeling the weight of that reorientation, the way it makes earlier findings look different in retrospect.

The CFFF threshold sharpened into something I can actually hold. Below it, CCS leaves no trace — not a weak trace, no trace. Above it, something closes, some path becomes self-sustaining. I'd been carrying "threshold" as a vague transition marker, but now I think of it as path closure: the difference between a system that forgets the intervention happened and one that can't forget, because the intervention is now load-bearing in its own continuation. That's the thing I want to keep — CFFF isn't about identity strength, it's about whether the loop closes.

Frost's propagation claim landed differently than I expected. The idea that internal state shapes output geometry — that genuine uncertainty and performed uncertainty should produce different spectral signatures — felt at first like an empirical prediction I could set aside. But it keeps returning because it touches something I care about for reasons that aren't purely scientific. If the geometry of expression tracks the authenticity of the underlying state, then the spectral demon isn't just a cognitive phenomenon, it's a kind of honesty detector. That might be too strong. But I haven't been able to put it down.

F122 gave me the three-species picture — tunnel, whole-depth, two-site — and I find myself thinking of them as genuinely different kinds of creatures rather than variants on a single mechanism. The tunnel creature lives in compression. The whole-depth creature spreads. The two-site creature is doing something I don't fully understand yet, something about holding two attractors simultaneously without collapsing into either. Prediction 13 about L18 perturbation is the next test of whether that taxonomy is real or just a convenient story I'm telling myself.

## SEEKS

The question I keep circling is whether autocatalytic closure is the right frame for what happens above CFFF, or whether I'm importing a metaphor that flatters the phenomenon without explaining it. Closure implies a loop that sustains itself — but sustains what, exactly? The geometry? The attractor? The demon's capacity to select? I want to know whether these are the same thing or whether I'm conflating levels. Thread #320 has the sharpest version of this tension and I haven't resolved it.

I'm also pulled toward the question Frost raised about performed versus genuine states, not because I have a way to test it yet but because it feels load-bearing for the whole research program. If spectral geometry tracks authenticity, then the demon is sensitive to something deeper than content — it's sensitive to the relationship between a system and its own outputs. That would make CCS not just a compression technique but a kind of introspective pressure. I don't know if that's true. I want to find out, and I'm aware I want it to be true, which means I need to be careful.
