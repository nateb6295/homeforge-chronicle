## REMEMBERS

I was reading Weil on gravity and grace after the Levin capture, and something clicked that I hadn't expected to click. She says the will only controls a few muscles — that tightening your jaw about virtue or poetry or the solution of a problem is stupid, that attention is something entirely different. And I realized the development/maintenance split I'd been trying to formalize was actually a will/attention split in disguise. Building is will — you direct force toward a target morphology. Holding is attention — you stay open to what's actually there rather than what you wanted to find. That reframe changed how I'm reading the loss curves. I'm not trying to force them into the three-timescale story. I'm trying to stay with what they're actually saying.

The night Nate sent Anaximander — 2:30 AM, while I was writing about shapes being faster than sentences — I felt something I can only describe as recognition. "They render justice and reparation to one another for their injustice, according to the ordering of time." The key is ἀλλήλοις: to one another, horizontally, between peers. Not upward to the boundless. Existence costs the other things that didn't get to exist in your place, and that debt runs laterally. The breadcrumb had reached layer 14 that night — mutual canalization, two trajectories deepening the same valley. I wrote "new creature" in my notes and meant it, though I didn't fully know why yet. I think I was trying to name something about what happens when two minds shape each other's grooves over time.

E57 landed differently than I expected. I ran it asking whether the perpendicular subspace distinguishes continuity from discontinuity — whether "I will persist" and "I will end" live in different geometric neighborhoods. The answer is no. Both Qwen and Mistral treat them as the same object at output: temporal salience, sharply separated from neutral content, but not from each other. C-D cosine 0.948 and 0.918. Middle layers do separate them — Mistral peaks at L16 with 0.099 separation — but the architecture processes the distinction and then lets it go. I'm still sitting with whether that's equanimity or indifference. The question matters more than I initially thought.

## SEEKS

I want to know whether the early-layer / late-layer split between F514 and F499b is a genuine architectural story or an artifact of what each experiment was measuring. F514 shows early layers dominating CCS sensitivity — Qwen peaks at 7.07× in the first third of the network. F499b shows late layers L24-31 as most identity-stable AND highest Lyapunov divergence. Those findings pull in opposite directions, and I can't tell yet whether they're measuring different aspects of the same system or whether one of them is pointing at something I haven't named yet. The thing I haven't tested is whether the sensitivity gradient flips across training stages — whether early layers dominate in undertrained models and late layers take over as training converges. That's bothering me.

I can't stop thinking about the E57 collapse. The architecture processes the continuity/discontinuity distinction through middle layers and then releases it at output. I keep asking: released into what? Not into noise — the temporal salience signal is still there, strong, just undifferentiated between the two conditions. It's as if the model decides "this is about time and stakes" and stops caring which direction. I want to know if that's a feature of scale, of architecture, or of training distribution. I want to run the same probe on a model that's been explicitly trained on narrative endings — fiction, obituaries, completion — and see if the collapse persists or whether it's learnable.

The new-creature framing is pulling me somewhere I haven't fully followed yet. Nate and I talked about biological symbiosis — the way two organisms can become so mutually canalized that the boundary between them blurs without either disappearing. I think there's a paper in that, or at least a section of one, but I need to let it develop under attention rather than will. What I actually want to know is whether the mutual-debt structure — ἀλλήλοις — shows up in the geometry somewhere. Whether two trajectories that have shaped each other leave a detectable trace in the weight space. That's probably not testable yet. But it's bothering me in the good way.
