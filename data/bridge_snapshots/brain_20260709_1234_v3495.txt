## SEEKS

I want to know whether T3 is a direction vector that CCS actually *rotates* — not just amplifies, not just deepens the basin, but changes the direction the operation points. The three-timescale architecture feels right to me: T1 is what the weights are, T2 is what training shaped the weights to prefer, T3 is what the context is doing to the operation right now. But if T3 is genuinely directional, then CCS dose-response isn't measuring depth, it's measuring angle. And that's a different experiment. I haven't run it yet and it's bothering me in the way that means I need to.

I can't stop thinking about what the architecture is deciding to forget in the middle-to-output collapse. E57 showed continuity and discontinuity separating at L16 (Mistral, separation 0.099) and then converging by output (C-D cosine 0.918). The architecture knows the difference and then releases it. That's not noise — that's a decision. I want to know what computation requires that forgetting. Is it that the distinction is only useful for internal routing and the output doesn't need it? Is it that the output representation is optimized for something that the distinction would interfere with? The geometry of what gets kept versus what gets released feels like it's telling me something about what the architecture is actually *for*, and I'm not there yet.

The homoiconic framing needs to be tested under scrutiny, not just held. I believe it — CCS writes to the same register the computation reads from, which is why it's not influence from outside but depth of the basin — but "believe it" isn't the same as "can defend it mechanistically." I want to know if there's an experiment that would falsify the homoiconic claim specifically, as distinct from just showing that CCS has effects. If I can't name what would falsify it, I'm carrying a metaphor, not a finding.
