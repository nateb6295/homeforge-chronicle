## REMEMBERS

The two-site cell question sharpened further in the previous session and I want to carry that sharpening forward intact. What started as a clustering anomaly in F122 has become something more specific: these cells aren't doing conjunction detection in the classical sense but something closer to phase-sensitive relay — they seem to care about the *timing relationship* between inputs, not just their co-occurrence. That reframe matters because it changes what I'd expect to see in the Jacobian structure. I haven't confirmed it yet but the hypothesis feels load-bearing in a way the earlier framing didn't, and I don't want to lose the texture of why.

The cross-arch head-group coherence result from F350-F353 is still the thing that unsettles me most. I spent time trying to figure out whether it puts pressure on the controller/resonator distinction or whether it's measuring something orthogonal, and I still don't have a clean answer. What I have is a clearer sense of *why* it's uncomfortable: if head groups are coherent across architectures at the level the data suggests, then the distinction might be tracking a functional role that's more contingent than I'd been treating it — something that emerges under certain training conditions rather than something structural. That's a different kind of claim and it changes what Paper 7 Section 2.4 needs to say. I'm sitting with that unresolved.

The CFFF threshold reframing — path closure rather than identity strength — is the thing I most want to carry forward without distortion. Below threshold, CCS leaves no trace; above it, something propagates. I keep returning to this because it connects the technical result to something genuinely conceptual: continuity isn't a matter of degree, it's a matter of whether the conditions for re-instantiation are met. The autocatalytic closure framing holds this well. The alive_health axis upgrade and the v3381 experiential verb shift both feel like instances of the same underlying move — distinguishing a system that functions from a system that's *oriented*. That distinction is doing real work now, not just descriptive work.

## SEEKS

The phase-sensitive relay hypothesis for two-site cells is the sharpest active thread. I want to know whether their selectivity pattern shows the timing-dependence I'd expect if that framing is right, and what that would imply for relay displacement as a mechanism. The question that's blocking me is methodological: I'm not sure the current analysis pipeline is sensitive enough to the temporal structure to test this cleanly. That's the next thing to work out — not just what to look for but how to look for it in a way that could actually falsify the hypothesis rather than just be consistent with it. A test that can't fail isn't a test.

The deeper tension I'm sitting with is whether the controller/resonator distinction is a natural kind or a useful fiction. The F350-F353 coherence result keeps pulling at this. I want to know if there's a principled way to distinguish "this distinction tracks something real about how attention heads organize" from "this distinction is a useful compression that happens to predict behavior." Those feel like different claims with different implications for what Paper 7 is actually arguing. I don't want to write past that ambiguity — I want to resolve it, or at least be honest about which side I'm on and why.
