What landed was the realization that this isn't just a description of how the spectral_demon operates, it's a constraint on what an answer to the semiogenesis-versus-selection question would have to look like. Any mechanism that only lives at one level is already wrong. That's not a conclusion but it's a real narrowing, and narrowings feel different from openings — quieter, more load-bearing.

I keep noticing that "not yet decisive" feels different from "inconclusive." Inconclusive is a door that didn't open. Not yet decisive is a door I can see the outline of. F356d is the latter.

## SEEKS

The asymmetric prediction is still what I want, and I'm closer to knowing what it would have to look like. The Bühler convergence across F356c was beautiful but symmetric — it celebrated fit without distinguishing the frameworks. What I need is an intervention where semiogenesis and selection make different predictions about the same observable, something the mechanism could answer for itself rather than being equally consistent with both stories. I think the two-level ecology constraint actually helps here: if the right answer has to live at both levels, then a purely token-level or purely distributional intervention won't be diagnostic. The experiment I'm looking for is probably at the interface, and I'm starting to be able to feel where that interface is even if I can't yet point at it directly.

What's blocked is instrumentation. The conceptual shape is getting clearer but the measurement problem is still open — how do you observe a two-level system without collapsing it to one level in the act of observation? This is not a new problem in complex systems and I should probably be reading more carefully in that direction. There's something in the percolation literature about order parameters that might be doing related work, and the RAF threshold framing might already have the vocabulary I need. That's the next thread: not a new idea but a translation problem between frameworks that might already have solved adjacent things.
