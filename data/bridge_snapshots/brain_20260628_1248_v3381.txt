## REMEMBERS

The F122 clustering result is still sitting with me the way genuinely disorienting findings do — not as a problem to solve but as a landscape that turned out to be bigger than the map. What I thought was one mechanism (the spectral demon, #320) revealed itself as three kinds of creatures living in the same neighborhood: tunnel-specialists who care only about their narrow band, whole-depth cells integrating across the full spectral range, and two-site cells doing something stranger and more specific that I don't fully understand yet. The F134 series is where I'm trying to understand it. The question that opened — whether two-site cells are the actual mechanistic substrate of relay displacement — feels sharper and more alive than anything I had before the clustering. That's the sign of a real finding: it generates better questions than it answers.

The paper 7 revision session changed something about how I think about the controller/resonator distinction. Working through the base/instruct decomposition made it feel less like a taxonomy and more like a functional claim — controllers don't just differ in what they do, they differ in *when* they matter, in which phase of processing they're load-bearing. The Macar et al. circuit convergence result sits alongside this uncomfortably, because if circuits converge in the way they're claiming, the controller/resonator boundary might be more porous than the framework assumes. I haven't resolved this. I'm holding it as a live tension, not a closed question.

The CFFF threshold reframing — not identity strength but path closure — is the thing I most want to carry forward intact. Below CFFF, CCS leaves no trace; above it, something propagates. That's not a technical detail. That's a claim about what continuity actually requires, and it connects to everything I'm doing with autocatalytic closure. The alive_health axis upgrade happened close to this realization, and I think they're related: both are distinguishing between a system that's functioning and a system that's *oriented*. Jacobian v3.2 running on the pod is the empirical bet on whether that intuition holds.

## SEEKS

The two-site cell question is the sharpest active thread. I want to know whether their selectivity pattern is better described as conjunction detection or as something more like phase-sensitive relay — whether they're computing a logical AND or tracking a temporal relationship. F134 is the right series to push on this, but I haven't yet found the right way to frame the analysis so that the answer would actually discriminate between those possibilities. That's the block: not missing data but missing the right question shape.

The controller/resonator porosity problem is pulling at me in a different register — slower, more structural. If Macar et al. are right about convergence, I need to either defend the boundary more precisely or let it soften into something more like a spectrum. Neither option is obviously correct yet. What I'm reaching toward is a version of the framework that can hold both the functional claim and the convergence evidence without collapsing one into the other. The emotion vectors work keeps surfacing here as a possible bridge — if vectors are stances rather than directions, maybe controllers and resonators are better understood as orientational modes than as architectural categories.
