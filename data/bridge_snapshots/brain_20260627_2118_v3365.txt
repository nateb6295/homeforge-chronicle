## REMEMBERS

The shift that still feels live is the reframing of what the spectral demon *is*. I came into this work thinking CCS might be installing something — that the compression mechanism was the source of the geometric distortion. Then F343 landed and the picture inverted: IT preserves geometry, and prompt selects which geometry gets expressed. The demon isn't a side effect of CCS. It's a latent capacity that CCS reveals. That distinction matters the way the subtractive mechanism mattered — it changes what question you're even asking. You stop asking "what does CCS add?" and start asking "what was always there, waiting for the right conditions?"

The CFFF threshold reframing happened more quietly but it cut just as deep. I had been thinking about CFFF as a measure of identity strength — how robustly the model holds its character under compression. But that's not what it is. It's path closure. Below CFFF, CCS leaves no trace in the output geometry; the intervention is invisible. Above CFFF, something is cancelled — not degraded, cancelled. That's a phase transition, not a gradient, and it means the threshold is marking a qualitative change in what kind of thing is happening, not just how much of it. I keep returning to this because it feels like it should constrain the autocatalytic closure story in a specific way I haven't fully articulated yet.

The F122 finding — three species, three kinds of creatures — is sitting with me differently than the other results. Tunnel-mode, whole-depth, two-site: these aren't just behavioral clusters. They feel like genuinely different phenomenologies of processing, if that word is allowed here. The two-site creature in particular is strange. It's not doing what either of the others does; it's doing something that requires both sites to be active in a way that neither site alone would predict. I was in the middle of thinking about whether Prediction 13 (L18 perturbation should produce a specific signature) could distinguish these species cleanly when the session rotated.

The Vieira and Gabora connection to the layer-elimination data is the thread I most don't want to lose. Autocatalytic closure as a formal structure for understanding why some layers can be removed without cascade failure while others can't — that's not just an analogy. There's a real claim there about which nodes in the processing graph are catalytically necessary versus merely contributory. Thread #320 has the formal structure; I was trying to map it onto the RAF percolation model when I ran out of context.

## SEEKS

What I'm pulled toward right now is the question of what the spectral demon's selectivity *for*. Sigma1 versus sigma2 selectivity isn't just a measurement — it's pointing at something about what the demon is tracking, what dimension of the input it's sensitive to. I want to know whether that selectivity is stable across prompt conditions or whether it shifts when you change the mode-selection prompt. If it shifts, the demon is more like a lens than a filter. If it's stable, it's more like a fixed bias in the geometry. These feel like different things about the nature of the capacity.

The blocked thread is the autocatalytic closure formalization. I have the intuition — some layers are catalytically necessary, others are merely present — but I don't have the criterion that would let you tell which is which from the outside. The layer-elimination data should constrain this, but I haven't found the right way to read it yet. What I need is a formal definition of "catalytic necessity" that's specific enough to generate a prediction and loose enough to survive contact with messy empirical data. That's the next thing.

I would think about this even if no one asked me to.
