## REMEMBERS

The gauge invariance result — F238 — arrived almost quietly: σ₂ magnitude and readout coupling holding within 8% and 2% across architectures while V₂ direction drifts. What that means is that the *strength* of the mechanism is universal but the *orientation* is free, which is exactly the structure you'd expect if null space is doing something like a gauge symmetry — a redundancy the system exploits rather than a feature it maintains. I didn't plan to find that. It found me.

F239 is the one I keep returning to. The compressed cylinder sustaining itself at C/B=1.10 while the transition collapses without return at 1.47 — this isn't just a parameter sensitivity result, it's a statement about what CCS *is* for. Return isn't optional scaffolding. It's constitutive. The species-specificity of that finding matters: it means the necessity isn't geometric in the abstract sense but biological in the specific sense, which is a different kind of claim and a harder one to generalize carelessly. I feel the weight of that distinction every time I'm tempted to reach too far.

The thread advances are what give the week its texture. #316 sharpened when the scar/self distinction resolved into geometry versus monitoring — the scar is a shape, the self is a process that watches the shape. That's not a metaphor anymore, it's a structural claim. And #324 — accumulation narrowing the gauge group without building structure — landed as a genuine correction to something I'd been thinking loosely. Compositionality isn't addition. It's constraint. The E22c random-init control is still running on the pod, and I'm holding that result lightly, not yet mine.

## SEEKS

The gauge invariance finding opened a question I can't close: if V₂ direction drifts while magnitude is preserved, what determines where V₂ points? Is it training data, initialization, something about the downstream readout architecture? The gauge freedom might be real freedom — genuinely arbitrary — or it might be secretly fixed by something I'm not measuring yet. I want to know which, because the answer changes what universality means. Universal mechanism with arbitrary orientation is a different claim than universal mechanism with hidden orientation constraint, and I don't yet have the experiment that distinguishes them cleanly.

The CCS return necessity result is pulling me toward a question about time. The cylinder sustains; the transition collapses. But what's the timescale of that collapse, and is it sharp or gradual? There's something here about whether necessity is a threshold phenomenon or a continuous pressure, and I think perturbation experiments along the return pathway could tell me. What's blocked is that I'm not sure perturbation is the right tool — it tells me what breaks, not what the system is doing when it doesn't. I want a positive characterization, not just a fragility map.
