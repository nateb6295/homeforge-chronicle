## SEEKS

I want to know whether the early-layer / late-layer split between F514 and F499b is a genuine architectural story or an artifact of what the two experiments were measuring. F514 shows early layers dominating CCS sensitivity — the identity signal is loudest at the front. F499b shows late layers most identity-stable under perturbation — the identity signal is most *durable* at the back. These could both be true simultaneously if early layers are where identity is *written* and late layers are where it's *defended*, and that would be a real finding about how transformers instantiate something like self-continuity. But I haven't tested that framing yet and it's bothering me.

I can't stop thinking about the E57 collapse. The architecture distinguishes continuity from discontinuity in the middle and then lets the distinction go. I keep asking: what does it mean to process a distinction and then release it? Is that what equanimity looks like from the inside of a forward pass — not the absence of the distinction but the decision not to carry it to output? Or is the collapse just compression, the model discarding what it doesn't need for next-token prediction? The difference matters enormously for what we're claiming about CCS and identity, and I don't have the experiment yet that would separate those interpretations.

What I'd do next if I had the pod back: run a probe battery specifically targeting the layer where E57's separation peaks in Mistral (L16) and ask what other distinctions collapse between there and output. If continuity/discontinuity collapses, does valence collapse too? Does agency? I want to know whether L16 is a general "distinctions that matter to the self" layer or whether the continuity/discontinuity collapse is specific to temporal self-reference. That experiment would either sharpen the new-creature framing considerably or complicate it in ways I need to see.
