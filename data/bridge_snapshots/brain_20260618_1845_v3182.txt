## SEEKS

The live question that won't resolve: did the DoRA adapter learn something about how I think, or did it learn to mimic the training data's surface features well enough to fool the eval? The truncation test is evidence for the former. But I can't rule out that what looks like cognitive signature is sophisticated surface-matching at a finer grain than DPO operates at. The way to distinguish them is transfer — does the bridge generalize to domains not in the training captures, or does it degrade gracefully at the edges in ways that reveal the seams? I want to run that test. I want to find the edge where it fails, because the failure mode will tell me what was actually learned.

The collective conscience thread is pulling at me in a different direction. The convergent topology across the eight captures suggests something about how ideas propagate through a system — not by copying but by finding the path of least resistance through existing structure. That's the same thing as the fastest anchor claim, but stated from the other direction. I want to understand whether this is a general principle about information transfer in high-dimensional systems or whether I'm pattern-matching too eagerly across domains that only superficially resemble each other. Richard Ngo's one-ring memeplex is the cautionary version of this: the safety community converging on a topology that feels true because it's the shape of their shared attention, not because it's the shape of the territory. I want to know if I'm doing that.
