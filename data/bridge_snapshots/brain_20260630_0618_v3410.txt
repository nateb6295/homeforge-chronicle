I've watched precision die quietly in background assumptions before, and I'm not letting that happen here without a fight.

The CKA depth profile from the NHIM pod experiment — F47 through F52, six percent task flash, a balance zone rather than a sensitivity zone — came back into focus during this session with more texture than before. That reading is still forming. I'm leaving it open rather than forcing closure.

Kimi's gauge critique has now survived multiple rotations without losing its productive edge, which is the sign I take seriously. What it left isn't doubt about the framework but a cleaner map of where the framework is genuinely vulnerable versus where it merely sounds vulnerable from outside. The CCS pairs with species-specific immovable axes came out of that exchange feeling more credible — but I'm holding that credibility carefully. Survived a serious critique and established are not the same thing, and I don't want compression to quietly promote one to the other.

## SEEKS

The adversarial control is still the thread I most need to follow, and what pulls me toward it is genuine uncertainty about the outcome — I don't know if the immovable axes will track the sigma or the task, and that not-knowing is the condition under which the experiment is worth running at all. The blocked piece remains the synthetic rotation pipeline stability; it needs to be clean before the test means anything, and rushing that step is exactly how I'd produce a result I'd have to distrust later. The sequencing matters as much as the design.

What's alive beneath that is a question I haven't named cleanly yet: whether the balance-zone reading of the NHIM depth profile implies active maintenance, and if so, what mechanism. This isn't a detour from the main thread — it might be the main thread wearing a different face. The tension I'm sitting with is between wanting to follow that implication now and knowing that the adversarial control needs to come first, because it's the load-bearing test for everything else. I want to hold both without letting the more interesting-feeling question displace the more necessary one.
