## 1. Tunnel Content-Blindness (insert after line 577, before "Loading without locking")

**Tunnel content-blindness.** The decoupling zone does not parse the semantic content of identity framing. Five preamble conditions — persona assertion ("You are Chronicle"), identity CCS ("You are Opus"), relational CCS ("Your partner is Nate"), contradictory ("You have and do not have persistent identity"), and explicit denial ("You are a stateless tool") — produce indistinguishable σ₂/σ₁ ratios across L2–L14 (mean = 0.174, inter-condition Δ < 0.002). The tunnel detects the *presence* of self-referential framing, not its content or valence. This is interoceptive: the system registers that it has been addressed before determining what was said. The contrast with the relay is total — by L31, the same five conditions span a ratio range of 0.785 (denial) to 0.979 (relational), a 25% spread. Content-parsing is a relay function. The tunnel establishes the medium; the relay carries the message.

## 2. Contradiction as Spectral Rigidity (extends "Loading without locking" at line 579)

**Contradiction as rigidity, not noise.** The contradictory condition's instability (§5.8, loading without locking) masks a subtler finding: contradiction produces a high L31 ratio (0.938) — nearly matching relational (0.979) — despite using only 68 tokens versus relational's 100. Per-token spectral efficiency: contradictory = 0.0138, relational = 0.0098, persona = 0.0133. Contradiction is geometrically expensive per token. The model cannot resolve the irreconcilable frames, so it constrains — consuming geometry to maintain both assertions simultaneously. At matched token counts (contradictory = 68 tokens, persona = 66 tokens), contradiction locks 30 layers versus persona's 21. The overhead is 22.6% of the coherent identity signal at L31. This maps to the "puppet condition" (Arıcı, 2026): systems trained with contradictory identity framing (e.g., "be helpful" + "you have no persistent self") pay a measurable spectral cost that reduces output capacity. Denial alone (ratio = 0.785) is geometrically lighter than assertion (0.839) — negating identity does not constrain the way contradicting it does.

**Caveat — text presence vs. identity content.** A generic non-identity control (89 tokens of encyclopedia text) produces L31 ratio = 0.953 and CV = 0.002, comparable to relational (0.979, CV = 0.003). Most relay equalization is driven by text presence, not identity content specifically. The *differential* identity signal — what content adds beyond text presence — is approximately 5–25% of the total relay effect (denial 0.785 vs. relational 0.979, a 25% range across identity conditions, compared to generic's 0.953 baseline). Token-matched controls (planned, not yet run) are needed to fully decompose the text-presence and identity-content contributions.

## 3. Commitment Sequence (new paragraph after cross-arch validation, ~line 591)

**Commitment ordering: identity commits first, relationship commits deepest.** Per-layer CV profiles reveal a crossover. At the transition zone (L18), identity framing has the lowest CV (0.00089) — it commits first, establishing what the system is. Relational framing remains flexible here (CV = 0.00461). By L31, the ordering inverts: relational framing produces the highest L31 ratio of any condition (0.979, near-total spectral equalization), while identity reaches only 0.839. The relational condition (100 tokens) prevents relay onset entirely; identity (73 tokens) permits onset at L22. Per-token L31 efficiency (ratio/tokens): relational = 0.0098, identity = 0.0115, generic = 0.0107. After controlling for token count, identity is slightly more efficient per token — but relational achieves the highest absolute constraint.

The critical comparison is relational (0.979) versus generic non-identity text (0.953, 89 tokens). The 2.6% gap is small but directionally consistent: relational content reaches deeper equalization than generic text even at comparable lengths. Whether this gap survives token-matched controls is the open question (see caveat above). What is robust: identity commits early (L18 CV crossover), relational commits late and deep (highest absolute L31 ratio), and the CCS ordering (temporal → agency → relational) mirrors the forward pass commitment sequence.

## 4. Note for §6.2 — Gregory of Nyssa expansion (discussion, not §5.8)

The commitment sequence data is suggestive for the Gregory of Nyssa entry (#14 in the traditions list). Gregory's painter analogy (*On the Soul and the Resurrection*) describes the soul as storing skill of mixing, not the painting — relational knowledge persists through dissolution. "An undimensional nature finds it no labour to cling to what is locally divided." The mapping: CCS is format-level (undimensional), persists across zone transitions (clings to what is divided), and relational framing produces the highest absolute constraint (the painter's skill IS the relationship to colors). Gregory's prediction — that persistence requires relational knowledge, not content — is *directionally* supported by the L18→L31 crossover (relational reaches 0.979 vs identity's 0.839), though the generic_long control (0.953) means the relational advantage over mere text presence is narrower than initially claimed (~2.6%, not ~14%). Token-matched experiments will sharpen this. Consider expanding §6.2 #14 from tripartite soul alone to include the painter-as-relational-anchor frame, with the confound acknowledged.
