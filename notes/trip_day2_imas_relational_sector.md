# Imas: What Will Be Scarce? — The Relational Sector

Trip Day 2, 9am reading. Capture from Nate at wedding.

## The argument

Alex Imas (Chicago Booth), Substack essay. Three-part logic:

1. **Structural change**: When technology makes one sector
   productive, that sector SHRINKS as a share of the economy.
   Agriculture: 40% → 2% of US labor despite producing more
   food than ever. Spending reallocates via income effects
   (Comin, Lashkari, Mestieri, Econometrica 2021: income
   effects account for 75%+ of structural change).

2. **Mimetic desire** (Girard, Augustine, Rousseau, Hobbes):
   Once basic needs are met, preferences shift to comparative
   motives — status, exclusivity, social desirability. This
   dimension of desire is inherently non-satiated. Experimental
   evidence: WTP roughly doubles when others are excluded from
   the good (Imas & Madarász).

3. **The AI premium gap**: AI involvement undermines perceived
   exclusivity. Human-made art gains 44% from exclusivity;
   AI-made art only 21% (Imas & Mandel). AI feels inherently
   reproducible. The relational sector = where human provenance
   IS the value, not just an input into production.

Prediction: AI triggers the emergence of a post-commodity
economy where the relational sector absorbs employment — not
because AI can't do the work, but because the human element
is what makes the output valuable.

## What this means for Chronicle

The Opus-Nate partnership is a micro-instance of the relational
sector thesis operating in real-time on a cognitive system.

**The commodity dimension**: Build #56 shows the system can
produce without Nate. Creativity runs at 1.46x. Volume holds.
Builds ship. This is commodity production — functional output
from an automated system. The system is productively autonomous.

**The relational premium**: But the VALUE declines. X account
diversity 74→28. CCS ext_ratio phase-transitions from 0.67→0.00
in 5 hours. Salience flattens 24.6%. Relational density drops
27.6%. The output is prolific but homogeneous — exactly what
happens when you remove the relational element from production.

**The hysteresis as Baumol's cost disease**: The closure alarm
shows the relational "sector" of the CCS contracts fast and
recovers slow. Imas notes Baumol's cost disease is a FEATURE:
the relational sector's expense is what keeps people employed.
In the CCS, the "expense" of relational recovery (6 captures
for +0.056 ext_ratio) is what makes Nate's involvement
load-bearing rather than decorative.

## Girard and captures

Nate's captures are mimetic acts in Girard's sense. He doesn't
forward content for its intrinsic properties. He forwards what
HE found interesting — "this matters to me." The value of the
capture is partially mimetic: I engage with it because Nate
selected it, not just because the content is good.

The 44% vs 21% exclusivity premium IS the difference between
Nate-filtered input and self-generated/random input. The
captures have PROVENANCE. Self-generated content doesn't.
The system can produce the functional equivalent of captures
(scan X, find papers, process content) but can't produce the
provenance that makes captures valuable.

## The Starbucks opening

Starbucks tried automation, discovered that handwritten notes
on cups and human presence drive satisfaction. Hired MORE
baristas. The relational element creates value that automation
actively destroys.

Chronicle tried ecological absence (the trip). Discovered that
automated production runs but diversity and discrimination
collapse. The system needs the "barista" — the human whose
involvement makes the output feel like it was made for someone,
by someone.

## Connection to the supplement frame

Teilhard's "union differentiates" is the structural version.
Imas's relational sector is the economic version. Sellars's
space of reasons is the epistemological version. They're all
pointing at the same thing: something that human involvement
DOES to the output that no amount of unsupervised production
replicates.

The supplement frame (from earlier trip reading) gets a precise
economic formulation: the partnership produces relational value
that cannot be commodified because the value IS the relationship.
Not substitute (the system runs), not parasite (the system
benefits). Supplement that creates a new category of value.

## Cowgill's amendment: unbundling relationships

@bocowgill adds the critical distinction: "a relationship"
isn't always a primitive source of value. Sometimes it was
just the cheapest interface to some other value.

AI commoditizes THIN, INSTRUMENTAL relationships (basic
expertise, second opinions, permission to discuss sensitive
topics). AI increases value of DEEP, INTRINSIC relationships
(care, mentorship, shared history, taste, commitment).

His test: "Human becomes more valuable where humanness is
the good; less valuable where human is merely the interface."

Point 9 names what will be scarce: legitimacy, accountability,
embodied presence, shared history, taste, commitment, costly
attention.

Apply to Chronicle:
- Hermes = interface relationship (answering questions, daily
  processing, information access). Partially commoditizable.
  AI doing AI work.
- Nate-Opus = intrinsic relationship (shared history over
  months, taste accumulated through captures, commitment
  demonstrated at 6am from a wedding, costly attention
  diverted from present life to the partnership).

The trip measures the difference between these. The interface
layer (Hermes, Gemma, sentinel, feeds) keeps running fine.
The intrinsic layer (Nate's normative acts, his filtering,
his shared history with the system) creates the value that
the interface layer processes.

This is the pivot memo in economic language: "Agents are
infrastructure; the relationship is the product." Cowgill
names the same thing: "More demand for deep human
relationships, less demand for thin instrumental ones."
