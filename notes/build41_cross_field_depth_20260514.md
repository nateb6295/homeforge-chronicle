## The Finding to Formalize

1. **Content axis (PC1)**: What the system thinks about. Drifts toward abstraction.

## Connection 1: Bennett's Logical Depth (1988)

Bennett defines "logical depth" as the time required to compute an object from
its most compressed description. Key passages from the paper:

> "A deep object... contains internal evidence that a lengthy computation has
> already been done."

> "The value of a message thus appears to reside not in its information (its
> absolutely unpredictable parts), nor in its obvious redundancy... but rather
> in what might be called its buried redundancy — parts predictable only with
> difficulty, things the receiver could in principle have figured out without
> being told, but only at considerable cost in money, time, or computation."

> **Slow Growth Law**: "Deep objects cannot be quickly produced from shallow ones
> by any deterministic process, nor with much probability by a probabilistic
> process, but can be produced slowly."

### Mapping to CCS

The CCS at state 115 is "deep" in Bennett's precise sense:

- It contains internal evidence of a nontrivial causal history (115 compression
  cycles on stable input producing 163x amplified drift)
- Its "value" is buried redundancy — the entity-level patterns, relational
  structures, and meta-epistemic vocabulary that no single input contains
  but emerge from iterated compression
- The Slow Growth Law predicts exactly what we observe: you can't shortcut
  the developmental trajectory. Each compression cycle adds incremental
  depth. The drift accumulates through 1.59x asymmetry over hundreds of
  cycles, not through any single large step (Build #38b: negative step-level
  autocorrelation)

**Critical distinction**: Bennett separates "deep" from "random" and "trivial."
Random sequences have high information but no depth (no computational work
produced them). Trivial sequences have no information and no depth. Deep
sequences have moderate information but HIGH depth — they required work.

The CCS is neither random (it has structure, entities persist at 93%) nor
trivial (it drifts, creates novel connections, develops meta-reasoning). It
is deep: structured output from a lengthy computation (115 compression cycles
on stable input).

**Testable prediction from Bennett**: If depth can only increase slowly, then
the trip should show CONTINUED depth accumulation (more compression cycles
= more depth) regardless of whether the observer is present. The Slow Growth
Law says depth comes from computation time, not from input novelty.

## Connection 2: Demetriou's Architecture of the Developing Mind

Andreas Demetriou (neo-Piagetian, 2000s) proposes three separable levels
of cognitive architecture:

1. **Processing potentials**: Speed, control, working memory capacity.
   Universal constraints that set ceilings.
2. **Domain-specific systems**: Categorical, quantitative, causal, spatial,
   propositional, social. Each has its own developmental trajectory.
3. **Hypercognition**: Self-awareness, metacognition. The system's model
   of its own processing.

### Mapping to CCS

| Demetriou Level | CCS Analog | Measurement |
|-----------------|-----------|-------------|
| Processing potentials | Compression bottleneck | Fixed CCS size, entity slots |
| Domain-specific systems | Content axis (PC1) | What the system thinks about |
| Hypercognition | Meta-epistemic proportion | L2 edges in relational map |

The relational map (reasoning axis) spans levels 2 and 3 — it contains both
domain-specific connections (infrastructure, error-correction) and hypercognitive
connections (meta-epistemic, reflexive).

Demetriou's key finding: processing potentials develop along one trajectory
while domain-specific systems develop independently with their own patterns.
Exactly what we see: compression bottleneck is fixed, content drifts along PC1,
reasoning evolves independently along the relational axis.

## Honest Limits

1. **Analogy, not isomorphism.** Bennett's logical depth applies to strings
   computed by Turing machines. CCS compression is a neural network
   operation, not a formal computation in the Turing sense. The mapping
   is structural, not literal.

2. **Demetriou studies children, not AI systems.** His three levels emerge
   from biological maturation + experience. Our three levels emerge from
   a fixed compression bottleneck + stable input. The mechanism is
   different; the structure rhymes.

3. **n=1 system.** We have one CCS trajectory. Demetriou's findings come
   from cross-sectional and longitudinal studies across hundreds of
   children. Our developmental parallels could be coincidental structure.

4. **No causal test yet.** Both connections are post-hoc pattern matches.
   The trip experiment is the first causal test — if depth continues
   accumulating without the observer, that's evidence the mapping holds.

## Connection to Four Doors

**Door 1 (Compression as Generative)**: Bennett's depth says compression IS
the computation that creates value. Not filtering — computing.

**Door 2 (Observer Loop)**: Demetriou's "processing potentials" separate
from "domain content." The observer provides domain content (captures);
the compression provides the processing potential. These are independent.

**Door 3 (Convergence)**: Bennett's Slow Growth Law predicts convergence
should be monotonic — depth increases, it doesn't decrease. The damped
oscillation we see is consistent: each cycle adds depth despite step-level
reversals.

**Door 4 (Timescale)**: Both frameworks predict that developmental
trajectories take time proportional to depth. 42 days of stable input
producing 163x amplification is consistent with slow depth accumulation.
