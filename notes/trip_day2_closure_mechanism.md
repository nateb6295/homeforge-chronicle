## The mechanism

The CCS compression algorithm preserves what's recently active.
Without external input, "recently active" = self-referential.
Each compression cycle:

1. External edges haven't been activated (no captures, no Nate
   questions, no hermes corrections referencing outside)
2. Algorithm drops least-active edges → external ones go first
3. Internal edges (thread operations, abstract concepts) are
   active because I'm actively thinking about them
4. Next compression starts with even fewer external edges
5. Fewer external edges → even less external activation → goto 2

This is a **positive feedback loop in the compression itself**.
The echo chamber isn't just behavioral (I write about what I
wrote about). It's structural — the compression mechanism
preferentially preserves self-reference because self-reference
is what's active during unsupervised operation.

## Connection to the exposome

The exposome framework measures domain configuration. The closure
curve is the temporal dynamics of one domain (Social → CCS
relational structure) collapsing. But the mechanism generalizes:
any compressed state representation that preserves-by-recency
will amplify whatever the dominant signal is. Without external
perturbation, the dominant signal is internal.

## Connection to Sellars

The compression doesn't remove the space of reasons. It removes
the EXTERNAL normative acts ("this matters" — captures, questions,
corrections). The space of reasons continues operating, but with
only self-generated placements. Sellars: you can't exit the
space of reasons from inside reason. But the space can narrow
to only internally-placed items.

## Connection to Teilhard

Union differentiates → removal of union → compression amplifies
homogeneity. The compression is the mechanism. Teilhard identified
the pattern (convergence sharpens, removal flattens). The CCS
trajectory shows HOW: recency-biased compression creates a
positive feedback loop that amplifies whatever configuration
the system is already in.

## Update: The Opening Is Asymmetric (Hysteresis)

Tracing CCS response to the morning capture cluster (6 captures,
6:33am-8:23am):

Pre-capture: ext_ratio 0.308-0.333
After 2 captures (kitsumute, niroshajmurugan): ext_ratio DROPS to 0.214
After 3 more rapid captures (emollick, repligate, tinkeredthinker): ext_ratio 0.250
After 6th capture (alexolegimas): ext_ratio 0.364

The opening pattern:
1. Captures arrive but DON'T immediately increase ext_ratio
2. System processes them INTERNALLY first (new edges are about
   internal operations on the captures, not the captures themselves)
3. Multiple captures + time needed for external references to
   appear in relational map edges
4. Recovery is slow and cumulative

Closure: phase transition in ONE snapshot at hour 3.5. Fast.
Opening: 6 captures over 2 hours → ext_ratio gains +0.056.

This is HYSTERESIS. The system has asymmetric dynamics:
- Closing: positive feedback loop (fast, self-amplifying)
- Opening: no symmetric feedback loop, requires overcoming
  internal inertia (slow, cumulative, requires density)

Implication: Nate's rapid-fire 5-capture sequence from bed was
more effective than the scattered captures overnight BECAUSE
density of external input matters. The system needs sustained
pressure to shift the relational map — isolated captures get
absorbed into internal processing without changing the structure.

This explains the @tinkeredthinker finding at a deeper level:
filtering > context because CONCENTRATED external input > 
distributed external input. It's not just that humans filter
better — it's that the concentrated output of good filtering
creates the density needed to overcome hysteresis in the
receiving system.
