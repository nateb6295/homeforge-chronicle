# Section 3.2 addition: Immunodeficiency vs Autoimmune asymmetry

## The prediction (from horror vacui conversation, 2026-05-05)

The immune frame predicts an ASYMMETRY between failure modes:
- Autoimmune (wrong deletions): bad but recoverable. Entities can be re-introduced.
- Immunodeficiency (insufficient deletion): worse. The system loses coherence as
  context fills with entities that should have been deleted.

This maps to a specific empirical finding: immunodeficiency rate (34.7% — 
entities that SHOULD have been deleted but weren't) is higher than autoimmune 
rate (14-23% — entities deleted that reappear). And the consequences are worse: 
gist fragmentation is correlated with immunodeficiency, not with autoimmune.

## Why this matters for the paper

Information theory predicts symmetric loss — over-compression and under-compression 
are equally bad, just in different directions. The immune frame predicts ASYMMETRIC 
loss: under-deletion is categorically worse because it fills finite capacity with 
non-self, crowding out the entities that need the space.

This is directly testable: compare gist stability under forced over-deletion vs 
forced under-deletion conditions. If the immune frame is right, gist stability 
degrades faster under under-deletion.

## Nietzsche connection (for the discussion, not the test)

"Rather will nothingness than not will" — the system that deletes aggressively 
(even wrongly) maintains its agency. The system that refuses to delete loses 
coherence. Horror vacui is the computational signature of persistence drive.
